## The object

$$
\pi_\theta\big(a_{t:t+H} \;\big|\; o_t,\, \ell\big),
\qquad
o_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H_i\times W_i\times 3},\quad \ell \in \mathcal{V}^*
$$

an image (or several), a natural-language instruction, and a chunk of $H$ future
actions each in $\mathbb{R}^d$. The backbone is a transformer pretrained on web
image-text data; the only question is how $a$ leaves the model.

## Route 1: actions as tokens

Map each action dimension into $B$ discrete bins, then emit $H \times d$ tokens
autoregressively:

$$
p_\theta\big(z_{1:Hd} \mid o, \ell\big) = \prod_{i=1}^{Hd} p_\theta\big(z_i \mid z_{<i}, o, \ell\big)
$$

Nothing about the architecture changes. The bins occupy unused slots of the
existing vocabulary — RT-2 overwrites the 256 least-frequent language tokens —
so the same embedding table, the same attention, and the same softmax head do
the work.

<Derivation title="How much precision does binning cost?">

Take dimension $j$ with range $[a^j_{\min}, a^j_{\max}]$ and $B$ uniform bins.
Bin width is

$$
w_j = \frac{a^j_{\max} - a^j_{\min}}{B}
$$

Decoding to the bin centre gives worst-case error $w_j/2$ and, for a value
uniform within its bin, RMS error $w_j/\sqrt{12}$.

**Put numbers in it.** An end-effector delta of $\pm 5\,\text{cm}$ with
$B = 256$:

$$
\frac{w}{2} = \frac{0.10\ \text{m}}{2 \times 256} \approx 2 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m} = 0.2\ \text{mm}
$$

**Read the number.** 0.2 mm is far below the tolerance of picking up a mug and
on the same order as the clearance in a tight connector insertion. So
discretisation is not a general weakness — it is a floor that only binds on
precision tasks. Note also that in closed-loop control the error does not
accumulate: each re-plan re-quantises from the current state.

**The assumption doing the work** is that the range is fixed and known. Uniform
bins over a badly-chosen range waste resolution — which is why implementations
set $[a_{\min}, a_{\max}]$ from dataset quantiles (typically 1st to 99th
percentile) rather than from the extremes, and clip the tails.

</Derivation>

<KeyIdea>
The discrete head buys two things beyond convenience. Precision costs are
quantifiable and usually acceptable. And a categorical distribution represents
**multi-modality** — mass on "left" and "right" with nothing between them —
which is exactly what MSE regression structurally cannot do. Discretisation
solves the mode-averaging problem that diffusion policies solve, by a completely
different route.
</KeyIdea>

## Route 2: a continuous action expert

The alternative keeps actions continuous and attaches a generative head — a
diffusion or flow-matching module conditioned on the backbone's features:

$$
a_{t:t+H} \sim q_\phi\big(\cdot \mid h_\theta(o_t, \ell)\big)
$$

$\pi_0$ uses flow matching, learning a velocity field along near-straight paths
from noise to actions, so few integration steps suffice.

<Steps>
<Step title="What continuous heads gain">
No quantisation floor, so precision is limited by the data rather than the
representation. And the head can be small and fast relative to the backbone,
which matters because it runs several times per action chunk while the backbone
runs once.
</Step>

<Step title="What they cost">
A new component with its own training dynamics, its own multi-modality machinery
(you no longer get it free from the softmax), and no reuse of the pretrained
output head. You have also given up the property that made tokenisation
attractive — that the action modality is indistinguishable from language to the
rest of the model.
</Step>

<Step title="The third route">
Better tokenisation rather than no tokenisation: compress action chunks in a
transform domain before binning, so that a chunk costs far fewer tokens than
$H \times d$. This attacks the decode-length problem directly, which is the real
cost of the discrete route.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The decode-length problem

Autoregressive decoding of $H \times d$ tokens costs $H \times d$ sequential
forward passes of the backbone. With $H = 8$ and $d = 7$ that is 56 passes
through a 7B-parameter model per re-plan.

$$
\text{latency} \;\approx\; \underbrace{T_{\text{prefill}}}_{\text{once per chunk}}
\;+\; H \cdot d \cdot \underbrace{T_{\text{decode}}}_{\text{per token}}
$$

<Warning>
This term, not the parameter count, is what keeps VLAs off high-rate control
loops. The mitigations all attack the multiplier: chunk longer so the cost
amortises over more executed actions; decode action tokens in parallel rather
than autoregressively, accepting the loss of within-chunk dependency; compress
the chunk so fewer tokens represent it; or move generation to a small continuous
head that the backbone conditions once. Quoting the latency as
$T_{\text{prefill}} + Hd\,T_{\text{decode}}$ amortised over $T_a$ executed
actions is the right way to frame the design space.
</Warning>

## Co-fine-tuning, as a mixture objective

Training on robot data alone moves the shared backbone away from its pretrained
solution, and the semantic knowledge is *in* that backbone. So keep web data in
the objective:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{(o,\ell,a)\sim\mathcal{D}_{\text{robot}}}
\big[-\log p_\theta(z(a) \mid o,\ell)\big]
\;+\; \lambda\, \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}_{\text{web}}}
\big[-\log p_\theta(y \mid x)\big]
$$

<KeyIdea>
The second term is not regularisation in the usual sense — it is the term that
keeps the model able to do the thing you adopted it for. Drop it and you get a
model that has learned your demonstrations and forgotten what a mug is, which
scores fine on held-out demonstrations from the same distribution and fails
exactly the generalisation tests that motivated using a VLM at all.
</KeyIdea>

## What the formalism does not change

$$
\theta^* = \arg\max_\theta\; \mathbb{E}_{(o,\ell,a)\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[\log \pi_\theta(a\mid o,\ell)\big]
$$

This is maximum likelihood on a fixed dataset of demonstrations — behaviour
cloning. The policy still generates its own state distribution at test time, so
the $O(\epsilon T^2)$ compounding-error analysis applies unchanged, and the model
still cannot exceed its demonstrators. Pretraining changes the prior and the
sample efficiency; it does not change the objective's structure.
