## Failure modes

Spatial reasoning failures: VLMs can identify objects but struggle with precise spatial relationships ('is the cup to the left or right of the bowl?'). Hallucination: confidently describe objects that aren't there. Symptoms: robot reaches for nonexistent objects or wrong locations. Fix: validate VLM outputs with detection/segmentation, use VLMs for high-level planning only (not precise spatial commands).

## Interview-ready answer

VLMs bring common-sense reasoning and language understanding to robot perception. The practical pattern: use VLMs for high-level scene understanding and task planning, then hand off to specialized modules for precise spatial reasoning and control. Key insight: VLMs are excellent at semantic understanding ('this is a cluttered kitchen with dishes to clean') but poor at precise spatial reasoning ('the mug handle is 2cm to the left'). In production, I combine VLM-based task planning with classical perception (pose estimation, point clouds) for execution.
