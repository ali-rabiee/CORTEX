## Formal definition

Large language models are autoregressive transformers trained to predict the next token: $P_\theta(x_t | x_{<t})$, pre-trained on internet-scale text corpora via the objective $\mathcal{L} = -\sum_t \log P_\theta(x_t | x_{<t})$. Key capabilities: (1) zero/few-shot task specification via prompting, (2) chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning — decomposing complex problems into intermediate steps, (3) instruction following via RLHF/DPO alignment. Key limitations for robotics: hallucination (generating plausible but incorrect physical facts), no grounded physical understanding (language about physics $\neq$ physics intuition), token limits bounding context window (typically 4K-128K tokens), and inability to directly perceive or act in the physical world without additional modules.
