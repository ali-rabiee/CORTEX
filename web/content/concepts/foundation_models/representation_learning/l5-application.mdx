## Failure modes

Representation doesn't capture task-relevant features: pre-trained on ImageNet (objects in context) but manipulation needs fine-grained spatial features (contact points, precise object pose). Symptoms: features don't discriminate between success/failure states. Fix: use robotics-specific pre-training (R3M, Voltron), fine-tune last layers, or train a small task-specific head.

## Interview-ready answer

Pre-trained visual representations are a game-changer for sample-efficient robot learning. Instead of learning to see and act from scratch, we use pre-trained encoders (R3M, DINOv2, CLIP) and learn only the policy head. In practice: frozen pre-trained encoder + small MLP policy trained on 50-100 demos works surprisingly well for many manipulation tasks. The choice of pre-training matters: R3M (human video) and Voltron (robot video) capture manipulation-relevant features better than ImageNet-pretrained models. Key trade-off: frozen encoders are sample-efficient but may miss task-specific features; fine-tuning the last few layers helps when you have enough data.
