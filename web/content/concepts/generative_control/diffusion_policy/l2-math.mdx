## The forward process

Fix an action chunk $a^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{T_p \times d}$ — the next $T_p$ actions,
flattened. Define a corruption chain with variance schedule
$\beta_1 \ldots \beta_K$, writing $\alpha_k = 1 - \beta_k$:

$$
q(a^k \mid a^{k-1}) = \mathcal{N}\big(\sqrt{\alpha_k}\, a^{k-1},\; (1-\alpha_k) I\big)
$$

The scaling by $\sqrt{\alpha_k}$ is not decoration: it keeps the variance from
growing, so that $a^K$ is standard Gaussian regardless of where you started.

<Derivation title="Collapsing the chain: $q(a^k \mid a^0)$ in one step">

Write the recursion with explicit noise draws,
$a^k = \sqrt{\alpha_k}\, a^{k-1} + \sqrt{1-\alpha_k}\, \epsilon_k$, and
substitute once:

$$
a^k = \sqrt{\alpha_k \alpha_{k-1}}\, a^{k-2}
 + \underbrace{\sqrt{\alpha_k(1-\alpha_{k-1})}\, \epsilon_{k-1}
 + \sqrt{1-\alpha_k}\, \epsilon_k}_{\text{two independent Gaussians}}
$$

Independent zero-mean Gaussians add in variance, so the bracket is a single
Gaussian with variance $\alpha_k(1-\alpha_{k-1}) + (1-\alpha_k) = 1 - \alpha_k\alpha_{k-1}$.
That is the same *form* as one step, with $\alpha$ replaced by the product.
By induction, with $\bar\alpha_k = \prod_{s \le k} \alpha_s$:

$$
\boxed{\;a^k = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}\, a^0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_k}\, \epsilon,
\qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\;}
$$

**The assumption doing the work** is that every forward transition is Gaussian
with a fixed, data-independent variance. That is what makes them composable at
all — and it is why the schedule must take small steps, since only for small
$\beta_k$ is the *reverse* transition also approximately Gaussian.

</Derivation>

## From the ELBO to the loss you actually implement

The reverse model is $p_\theta(a^{k-1} \mid a^k, o) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta(a^k, k, o), \sigma_k^2 I)$,
and the variational bound asks it to match the true posterior
$q(a^{k-1} \mid a^k, a^0)$, which is itself Gaussian with mean

$$
\tilde\mu_k(a^k, a^0) =
\frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{k-1}}\,\beta_k}{1-\bar\alpha_k}\, a^0
+ \frac{\sqrt{\alpha_k}\,(1-\bar\alpha_{k-1})}{1-\bar\alpha_k}\, a^k
$$

<Derivation title="Why the target is the noise, not the action">

The posterior mean above depends on $a^0$, which the sampler does not have. But
the boxed identity rearranges to
$a^0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}}\big(a^k - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_k}\,\epsilon\big)$.
Substituting and simplifying collapses the two terms into one:

$$
\tilde\mu_k = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_k}}
\left(a^k - \frac{\beta_k}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_k}}\, \epsilon \right)
$$

So the *only* unknown in the posterior mean is $\epsilon$. Parameterise
$\mu_\theta$ with the identical expression but $\epsilon_\theta(a^k,k,o)$ in
place of $\epsilon$. The KL between two Gaussians of equal variance is a scaled
squared distance between means, and everything cancels except:

$$
\mathcal{L}_k = \frac{\beta_k^2}{2\sigma_k^2\,\alpha_k(1-\bar\alpha_k)}
\;\big\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(a^k, k, o)\big\|^2
$$

DDPM then **drops the weight**, training the unweighted mean squared error:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} = \mathbb{E}_{(a^0,o) \sim \mathcal{D},\, k,\, \epsilon}
\Big[\big\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k} a^0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_k}\epsilon,\, k,\, o)\big\|^2\Big]
$$

</Derivation>

<Warning>
Be precise about that last step in an interview. $\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}$
is **not** the ELBO — it is the ELBO with a $k$-dependent weight replaced by a
constant. The weight peaks at small $k$, so dropping it shifts capacity toward
the high-noise steps that decide coarse structure. For a policy, coarse
structure is *which strategy this is*, which is precisely what you care about.
The maths says one thing and the samples say another; the field went with the
samples.
</Warning>

## The score view, and why modes survive

Differentiate the closed-form forward marginal:

$$
\nabla_{a^k} \log q(a^k \mid a^0)
= -\frac{a^k - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}\, a^0}{1-\bar\alpha_k}
= -\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_k}}
$$

So $\epsilon_\theta$ is a negatively-scaled estimate of
$\nabla_a \log p(a \mid o)$. Sampling is annealed gradient ascent on the
conditional log-density of expert action chunks: start anywhere, follow the
gradient, arrive at a local maximum.

<KeyIdea>
This is the whole multi-modality argument in one line. MSE regression minimises
a loss whose unique minimiser is $\mathbb{E}[a \mid o]$ — capacity cannot help,
because the mean is what the loss *asks for*. Score-following is local: two
seeds fall into two basins, and the basins are the modes. The network is still
a deterministic function; the sampler is what carries the distribution.
</KeyIdea>

## Two things that make it deployable

<Steps>
<Step title="DDIM: same marginals, fewer steps">
DDIM constructs a non-Markovian reverse process with *identical* forward
marginals, so a DDPM-trained $\epsilon_\theta$ can be reused unchanged. With
$\eta = 0$ the update is deterministic:
$a^{k-1} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{k-1}}\,\hat a^0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{k-1}}\,\epsilon_\theta$,
where $\hat a^0$ is the current clean-sample estimate. Because it only ever
refers to $\bar\alpha$, you can evaluate it on any increasing subsequence of
timesteps — ~10 instead of 100. Sample diversity does drop; that is the trade.
</Step>

<Step title="Receding horizon: predict $T_p$, execute $T_a$">
Predict a long chunk for temporal coherence, execute only its first
$T_a < T_p$ actions, then re-plan from a fresh observation. Chi et al. use
$T_p = 16$ with $T_a$ around 8. Long enough to be consistent, short enough to
stay reactive — the tuning knob between open-loop smoothness and closed-loop
responsiveness.
</Step>
</Steps>
