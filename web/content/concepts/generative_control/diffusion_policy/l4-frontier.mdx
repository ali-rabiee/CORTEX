## The diagnosis came first

<PaperCard refKey="florence2021ibc" />

Before there was a diffusion policy there was an argument: that the failure of
behaviour cloning on contact-rich tasks is not a data problem or a capacity
problem but a **loss** problem. An explicit policy trained with MSE is a
function fit to a conditional mean, and it cannot represent a discontinuity —
yet manipulation is full of them, because the right action either side of a
contact event can be completely different.

Implicit BC's fix was to learn an energy $E_\theta(o, a)$ and act by minimising
it over $a$. That represents modes and discontinuities cleanly. It also inherits
everything unpleasant about energy-based models: an objective that needs
negative samples, and an optimisation over action space at every control step.

DDPM's contribution to robotics is indirect but decisive: it turned a
variational-inference problem into `mse_loss(net(noisy, k, cond), noise)`. A
technique that simple gets tried on everything. DDIM then removed the objection
that would otherwise have ended the story — that 100 sequential network passes
per action is absurd for a controller.

## Diffusing behaviour

<PaperCard refKey="janner2022diffuser" />

Diffuser made the conceptual leap: a trajectory is just a 2-D array, so
generate it the way you generate an image. Planning becomes conditional
sampling, and goals or rewards enter as *guidance* on the sampler rather than as
an objective for a search. Elegant — but it diffuses states as well as actions,
which means predicting the future world, and the results lived on
low-dimensional offline-RL benchmarks.

<PaperCard refKey="chi2023diffusionpolicy" />

Diffusion Policy is what you get by asking which parts of that are actually
necessary for control. Three subtractions and one addition:

<Steps>
<Step title="Diffuse actions only">
Drop states from the diffused variable. The policy no longer has to model how
the world evolves — a demand that pixels make brutal — and the output is
directly executable.
</Step>

<Step title="Condition, don't inpaint">
Feed the visual observation in as conditioning to every denoising step instead
of fixing it as known entries of the diffused array. This is what lets a
visual encoder be trained end-to-end with the policy.
</Step>

<Step title="Execute receding-horizon">
Predict $T_p$ actions, execute $T_a$, re-plan. Closed-loop reactivity with
open-loop temporal consistency, tuned by one integer.
</Step>

<Step title="Then evaluate honestly, across 15 tasks">
The paper's real weight is not the architecture but the breadth of the
comparison — consistent gains over LSTM-GMM, IBC, and BET across simulated and
real benchmarks, which is what moved the community rather than a single hero
demo.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Where the pressure moved

<PaperCard refKey="black2024pi0" />

Once multi-modality was solved, the binding constraint became inference cost —
and both of these are answers to it. Consistency Policy distils the sampling
chain into a student that jumps most of the way in one step. $\pi_0$ takes the
other route: flow matching learns a velocity field along much straighter paths
from noise to data, so few integration steps are needed by construction.

<KeyIdea>
The thirty-second arc: 2021 diagnosed regression as the culprit and paid for it
with a fragile energy model; 2022 showed trajectories could be generated like
images; 2023 stripped that down to an action-only, observation-conditioned,
receding-horizon policy that runs on hardware; since then it has been a latency
race, and the generative action head has been absorbed into VLAs as a component
rather than surviving as a standalone method.
</KeyIdea>
