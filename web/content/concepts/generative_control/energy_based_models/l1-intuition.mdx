## Intuition

Instead of directly telling the robot what to do (explicit policy), an EBM defines a landscape over all possible actions — valleys are good actions, mountains are bad ones. The robot finds good actions by rolling a ball downhill on this landscape (gradient descent). The beauty: the landscape can have multiple valleys (multi-modal), and you can combine landscapes by addition (compositionality). Want an action that's both safe AND efficient? Just add the two energy functions.

## In practice

Implicit Behavioral Cloning (Florence et al.): instead of regressing $a = f_\theta(o)$, train an energy function $E_\theta(a, o)$ with InfoNCE loss: $\mathcal{L} = -\log \frac{\exp(-E_\theta(a^+, o))}{\exp(-E_\theta(a^+, o)) + \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{neg}}} \exp(-E_\theta(a_j^-, o))}$ where $a^+$ is the demonstration action and $a_j^-$ are negative samples. At inference: draw $N = 16384$ uniform action samples, evaluate energy on all, keep top-$k$, refine with Langevin dynamics. Achieves state-of-the-art on precise insertion tasks where multi-modal action distributions matter.
