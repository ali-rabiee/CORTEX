## Formal definition

Energy-based models (EBMs) define a policy implicitly through an energy function $E_\theta(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{o}): \mathcal{A} \times \mathcal{O} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ where low energy corresponds to good actions. The policy distribution is: $$p_\theta(\mathbf{a} | \mathbf{o}) = \frac{\exp(-E_\theta(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{o}))}{Z_\theta(\mathbf{o})}, \quad Z_\theta(\mathbf{o}) = \int \exp(-E_\theta(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{o}))\, d\mathbf{a}$$ Action inference requires sampling from this distribution, typically via Langevin dynamics: $\mathbf{a}_{k+1} = \mathbf{a}_k - \frac{\lambda}{2} \nabla_\mathbf{a} E_\theta(\mathbf{a}_k, \mathbf{o}) + \sqrt{\lambda}\, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_k$. Training uses contrastive divergence or noise contrastive estimation (NCE) to avoid computing the intractable partition function $Z_\theta$.
