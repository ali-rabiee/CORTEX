## Failure modes

Compounding errors in hierarchical policies: high-level planner generates a subgoal the low-level policy cannot reach (e.g., requires passing through an obstacle). Temporal abstraction mismatch: subgoals are too far apart (low-level fails to bridge) or too close (overhead of hierarchy without benefit). Trajectory diffusion scalability: generating trajectories of $T = 1000$ steps requires diffusing in a $1000 \times d$-dimensional space — model capacity and training data requirements scale poorly. Subgoal representation: pixel-space subgoal images are brittle to lighting/viewpoint changes; latent subgoals are hard to interpret and debug. Symptoms: robot completes first few subgoals then fails, generated trajectories drift from feasibility constraints, long plans are physically impossible in the middle. Fix: train the high-level policy with reachability-aware objectives (only generate subgoals the low-level can reach), use adaptive temporal abstraction (shorter subgoals for difficult segments), plan in a learned latent space rather than raw state space, implement re-planning at multiple hierarchy levels with feasibility checking.

## Interview-ready answer

Long-horizon generation is one of the hardest open problems in robot learning. The fundamental tension: you need to reason about the future (planning) while handling uncertainty and multi-modality (generative models). The approaches I find most promising: (1) hierarchical diffusion — high-level diffusion model generates subgoal sequences, low-level diffusion policy executes between them (SuSIE, VPDD), (2) trajectory-level diffusion — Diffuser and its variants generate full trajectories with inpainting for constraints, (3) plan-then-act with LLMs — language-based planning for task decomposition with learned low-level controllers for execution. The key design choice: subgoal representation. Image subgoals (SuSIE) are intuitive but brittle; latent subgoals are compact but hard to debug; language subgoals (SayCan) are interpretable but lose spatial precision. In practice, I advocate for a hybrid: language for task decomposition, latent or image subgoals for spatial precision within each sub-task, and replanning at both levels for robustness to execution errors.
