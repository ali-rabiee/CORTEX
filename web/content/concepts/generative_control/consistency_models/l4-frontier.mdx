## Halving, repeatedly

<PaperCard refKey="salimans2022progressive" />

The first practical answer, and still a good one. Train a student to reproduce
two of the teacher's steps in one, then treat that student as the new teacher and
repeat. Each stage is an ordinary regression with a well-defined target, which
makes it reliable — at the cost of a chain of full training runs to reach a
handful of steps.

## One stage instead of a chain

<PaperCard refKey="song2023consistency" />

The reframing: rather than compressing the solver step by step, define the object
you actually want — the map from any point on the trajectory to its endpoint —
and characterise it by self-consistency plus the boundary condition.

<Steps>
<Step title="Distillation form">
Use a pretrained diffusion model's solver to generate adjacent points on a
trajectory, and train the map to agree across them.
</Step>

<Step title="Standalone form">
Construct adjacent points from an unbiased score estimate instead, removing the
teacher entirely — which is the more surprising claim of the two.
</Step>

<Step title="The delicate part">
The target is an EMA copy of the network itself, so the objective compares the
model against a moving version of itself. That is what makes it powerful and what
makes it fragile.
</Step>
</Steps>

<PaperCard refKey="song2024improvedcm" />

The follow-up is worth reading precisely because it is unglamorous. Standalone
consistency training did not match distillation until the noise schedule, the
distance metric and the EMA treatment were reworked — so a substantial part of
whether the method works lived in choices the original framing did not
foreground.

## Why robotics wanted this

<PaperCard refKey="prasad2024consistency" />

<KeyIdea>
The step budget for a policy is set by the control loop, not by patience. That
changes the economics: paying for an extra training stage purely to reduce
inference cost is hard to justify for image generation and obviously worth it
when the alternative is missing a deadline. It is also why this cluster's three
routes — better integrator, straighter coupling, learned endpoint map — are worth
holding apart. The first is free and floors out around twenty evaluations; the
other two cost a training stage and reach one or two.
</KeyIdea>
