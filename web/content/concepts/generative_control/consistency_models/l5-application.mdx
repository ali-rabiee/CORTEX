## Failure modes

Quality gap: single-step generation is inherently less precise than multi-step refinement, especially for high-dimensional action spaces. The distilled model may struggle with the tails of the action distribution (rare but valid modes). Symptoms: slightly lower success rate on tasks requiring high precision (e.g., peg insertion with $< 1\,\text{mm}$ tolerance). Fix: use 2-4 step inference as a compromise (still much faster than full diffusion), train with larger model capacity, use progressive distillation schedule.

## Interview-ready answer

Consistency models solve the key deployment bottleneck of diffusion policies: inference speed. By learning to map any noise level directly to the clean sample (self-consistency property $f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) = f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t'}, t')$), we get single-step generation. For robotics: this means going from 10+ denoising steps ($\sim 500\,\text{ms}$) to 1-2 steps ($\sim 50\text{-}100\,\text{ms}$), enabling real-time closed-loop control. The training recipe: take a well-trained diffusion policy, distill with consistency loss using EMA target network. I see consistency models as the deployment form of diffusion policies — train with diffusion for quality, deploy with consistency for speed.
