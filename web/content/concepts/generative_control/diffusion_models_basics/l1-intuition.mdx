## Intuition

Imagine teaching someone to restore a painting by practicing on progressively more damaged versions. In the forward process, you systematically add damage (noise) until the painting is unrecognizable static. In training, you show the model paintings at every damage level and teach it to undo one small step of damage. At generation time, you start with pure static and iteratively restore it — each step removes a little noise, gradually revealing a coherent image (or action sequence). The score function tells you: 'from this noisy state, which direction leads toward clean data?' — it's literally the gradient of the log-probability landscape.

## In practice

DDPM for image generation: train a U-Net $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta$ on ImageNet, sample by iterating $\mathbf{x}_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(\mathbf{x}_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)) + \sigma_t \mathbf{z}$ for $T = 1000$ steps. DDIM acceleration: same trained model, but deterministic sampling with $\eta = 0$ in 50 steps — similar quality, 20x faster. For robotics: the same framework applies to action sequences — $\mathbf{x}_0$ is a trajectory of robot actions, the U-Net is conditioned on observations, and denoising produces a clean action trajectory. Noise schedule matters: cosine schedule $\bar{\alpha}_t = \cos^2(\frac{t/T + s}{1+s} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2})$ works better than linear for low-dimensional action spaces because it preserves signal longer in the forward process.
