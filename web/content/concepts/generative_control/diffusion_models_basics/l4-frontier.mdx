## The idea arrives early

<PaperCard refKey="sohldickstein2015thermo" />

The construction is already complete here: run a diffusion that destroys
structure, learn the reverse. What was missing was the practical form of the
objective and the scale to make it work — which is why the paper is worth citing
when someone dismisses an approach for currently underperforming.

## The unification

<PaperCard refKey="song2021sde" />

This is the paper the family view comes from. Two things follow from it:

<Steps>
<Step title="DDPM and score matching are the same thing">
Different discretisations of one continuous-time SDE, differing in $f$ and $g$
rather than in kind.
</Step>

<Step title="The probability-flow ODE has identical marginals">
So the sampler is separable from the model, and a deterministic path exists.
Everything after — DDIM, higher-order solvers, consistency distillation, the
bridge to flow matching — acts on that ODE.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Fixing the schedule, then the whole design space

<PaperGrid>
<PaperCard refKey="nichol2021improved" />
<PaperCard refKey="karras2022edm" />
</PaperGrid>

The cosine schedule reads as a detail and is not. Because timesteps are drawn
uniformly, the schedule determines the distribution of difficulties the network
trains on, and the linear schedule put over half of them where the optimal
prediction is nearly the prior.

EDM then did the more general thing: put the published variants into a common
parameterisation and showed that most of their apparent differences were
reparameterisations, leaving a small set of genuinely independent choices.

<KeyIdea>
The pattern across these four is that progress came from *separating* things that
had been fused. Model from sampler, schedule from parameterisation, training
difficulty from timestep index. Each separation turned a fixed property of an
algorithm into a knob you can turn after training — which is why the practical
advice for this family is so often "you can change that at inference time".
</KeyIdea>
