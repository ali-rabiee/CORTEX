## What it learns

<Intuition>
Diffusion learns a score, and the sampler recovers a velocity from it. Flow
matching skips the intermediate step and learns the velocity directly: a field
$v_\theta(x, t)$ whose integral curves carry the noise distribution onto the data
distribution. The two are close relatives — the probability-flow ODE of a
diffusion model is exactly such a field — but starting from transport rather than
from noising makes the path an explicit choice instead of a consequence of the
noise schedule.
</Intuition>

The training trick is what made it practical. The velocity you want is an
expectation over which data point the current state might be heading towards,
which you cannot evaluate. But *conditioned* on a data point, the velocity along
your chosen interpolant is closed-form algebra. The theorem is that regressing
the conditional target has the same gradient as regressing the intractable one —
the same manoeuvre that lets denoising score matching stand in for score
matching.

<KeyIdea>
So training never simulates a trajectory. Sample $t$, sample a data point, sample
a noise point, place yourself on the interpolant between them, and regress the
velocity you know analytically. This is what separates flow matching from earlier
continuous normalising flows, which had to integrate the ODE inside the training
loop.
</KeyIdea>

## The claim that does not survive measurement

The usual sales pitch is that flow matching uses straight paths, so you can take
few large steps. The first half is true and the second does not follow.

<Figure src="/figures/flow-path-straightness.svg" alt="Left: with an independent coupling the straight conditional paths cross, so the marginal trajectory bends — measured 21 percent off its own chord; after reflow the paths do not cross and the trajectory is straight. Right: error against evaluations, where linear interpolation is worse than the diffusion path at every budget while the rectified coupling sits at the noise floor after one step." caption="Straight conditional paths, curved marginal path. Fixing the coupling is what buys one-step sampling." />

Each conditional path *is* a straight line. But the sampler follows the
**marginal** field, which at any point averages over every conditional path
passing through it — and averaging directions that disagree gives a field whose
integral curves bend. On a one-dimensional mixture with the field computed
exactly, that trajectory deviates from its own chord by **21%** of the total
displacement.

Measured against a variance-preserving diffusion path on the same target:

| evaluations | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| diffusion | 1.20 | 0.303 | 0.063 | 0.027 | 0.015 | 0.0086 |
| linear interpolation | 2.00 | 0.636 | 0.244 | 0.123 | 0.062 | 0.031 |
| rectified coupling | **0.0036** | 0.0036 | 0.0036 | 0.0036 | 0.0036 | 0.0036 |

Plain linear interpolation is **worse** than diffusion at every budget. The
rectified version is at the sampling-noise floor of 0.0037 after a single step.

## Where one-step generation comes from

<Steps>
<Step title="The interpolant is not the problem">
Choosing a straight line between noise and data does not make the induced flow
straight, because you do not choose which noise sample pairs with which data
sample.
</Step>

<Step title="The coupling is">
Reflow runs the ODE once to obtain matched pairs, then retrains on the straight
line between each *matched* pair. Those paths no longer cross.
</Step>

<Step title="Then straightness is real">
With a non-crossing coupling the marginal trajectory is the straight line, so one
Euler step is exact rather than approximate.
</Step>

<Step title="And it costs a generation pass">
Reflow needs a full sampling run over the dataset to build the pairs, plus
retraining. One-step sampling is bought, not free — which is the same trade
consistency distillation makes by a different route.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Be careful with the phrase "flow matching is faster than diffusion". As a
statement about the interpolant it is not supported — the measurement above has
it losing. What flow matching genuinely provides is a simulation-free objective
and an explicit, editable path, which is what makes rectification expressible in
the first place. The speed comes from the rectification.
</Warning>
