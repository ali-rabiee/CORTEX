## The objective

<PaperGrid>
<PaperCard refKey="lipman2023flowmatching" />
<PaperCard refKey="albergo2023interpolants" />
</PaperGrid>

Two papers, one construction, reached from different directions. The shared
contribution is that a continuous normalising flow can be trained by regression,
with no integration in the loop — which is what moved CNFs from an elegant idea
with an awkward training story to something that scales.

Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden's framing is the more useful one for building things:
the interpolant between the two distributions is an explicit object you choose,
rather than a consequence of a noising process you inherited.

## The part that produces the speed

<PaperCard refKey="liu2023rectified" />

Worth being precise about, because the popular summary attributes rectified
flow's speed to its linear paths and that is not where it comes from.

<Steps>
<Step title="The interpolant is linear before and after">
So it cannot be the source of the improvement.
</Step>

<Step title="What changes is the coupling">
Reflow pairs each noise sample with the data sample the current ODE actually
sends it to, then retrains on those pairs.
</Step>

<Step title="Deterministic maps do not cross">
So the paths through any region agree, the averaged field equals the individual
velocities, and the trajectory is genuinely straight.
</Step>

<Step title="Then Euler is exact rather than approximate">
Which is what makes one step sufficient — measured at the sampling-noise floor,
against roughly 550× worse before rectification.
</Step>
</Steps>

## At scale

<PaperCard refKey="esser2024sd3" />

The systematic comparison, and a finding that should look familiar: how you
sample $t$ during training matters, and weighting the middle of the interval
beats uniform sampling.

<KeyIdea>
That is the cosine-schedule result in flow-matching coordinates. Both say the
same thing — the sampled parameter determines the difficulty of the regression
problem, the ends of the range are nearly trivial, and uniform sampling therefore
spends a large share of capacity where little is learnable. Seeing them as one
result rather than two is the kind of connection that makes the diffusion and
flow literatures navigable, and it is exactly what the "diffusion meets flow
matching" line of work argues in general.
</KeyIdea>
