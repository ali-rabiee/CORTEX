## Sample the mode, don't average it

<PaperCard refKey="lynch2019play" />

The earliest of these in spirit: put a latent variable in the policy so that
sampling it selects *which* way to accomplish the goal. The setting matters as
much as the model — unsegmented teleoperated play is maximally multimodal, since
nobody was following a script, so a policy that averages has nothing to say.

## Give up the point prediction

<PaperCard refKey="florence2021ibc" />

The clearest statement of the diagnosis: the regression head is the problem, not
the data. An energy $E(o,a)$ can be low at two well-separated actions and high
between them, which is exactly what a point prediction cannot do.

The reason it is not the standard answer today is about optimisation rather than
expressiveness. Training depends on how negatives are sampled, and inference
requires searching action space for the minimum. Diffusion reached comparable
expressiveness with a plain regression objective and a fixed-length sampler.

## Discrete choice, continuous precision

<PaperGrid>
<PaperCard refKey="shafiullah2022bet" />
<PaperCard refKey="lee2024vqbet" />
</PaperGrid>

<Steps>
<Step title="The decision is discrete, the action is not">
Choosing left or right is categorical; how far to turn is continuous. Forcing one
set of bins to serve both makes them fight — few bins for a clean choice, many for
precision.
</Step>

<Step title="So split them">
BeT clusters actions into $k$ centres, predicts a bin with a categorical head,
and regresses a residual offset within it.
</Step>

<Step title="Cluster joint actions, never per dimension">
The clustering is over whole action vectors. Binning each dimension separately
would reproduce every marginal and still place half the mass on combinations that
never occurred.
</Step>

<Step title="VQ-BeT learns the codebook">
Vector quantisation over action chunks replaces $k$-means, so the discrete units
are learned and cover short sequences rather than single steps.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
Four methods that look unrelated, making one move: replace the point output with
something representing a distribution over the whole action. That is forced
rather than aesthetic — every loss of the form $\mathbb{E}[\ell(a - f(o))]$ has a
minimiser that is a single summary of $p(a \mid o)$, so searching for a better
$\ell$ cannot work. Diffusion policies are the fifth entry in the same list, and
being able to place them in it is more useful than knowing any one of them well.
</KeyIdea>
