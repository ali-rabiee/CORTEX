## Intuition

Instead of predicting one action at a time (which leads to jerky, myopic behavior), action generation produces a whole trajectory segment at once — like planning your next several chess moves rather than just the next one. The denoising process starts with random noise shaped like an action trajectory and gradually sculpts it into a coherent, smooth sequence of actions that responds to what the robot currently sees. Executing only the first few steps and then replanning gives you the best of both worlds: temporal coherence from chunk generation and reactivity from frequent replanning.

## In practice

Diffusion Policy implementation: observation encoder (ResNet-18 on 2 camera views + proprioception MLP) produces conditioning vector $\mathbf{c} = [\phi_{\text{img}}(o_{t-1}), \phi_{\text{img}}(o_t), \phi_{\text{prop}}(q_t)]$. Temporal U-Net takes noisy action chunk $\mathbf{a}^s \in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times 7}$ (16 steps, 7-DOF) with FiLM conditioning from $\mathbf{c}$. Train with DDPM loss, sample with DDIM (10 steps). Execute first 8 of 16 predicted steps, then replan — the 8-step overlap provides smooth transitions between chunks. Running at 10Hz on an NVIDIA 3090, the system controls a bimanual ALOHA robot for folding clothes.
