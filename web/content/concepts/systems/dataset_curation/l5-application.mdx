## Failure modes

Distribution mismatch: training data collected in one setting (single table, good lighting) but deployed in another (different table height, variable lighting). Covariate shift causes policy failure on out-of-distribution observations. Also: demonstrator inconsistency — multiple operators with different styles create conflicting multi-modal data that confuses unimodal policies. Symptoms: policy works in the training environment but fails in deployment; policy exhibits hesitant or averaged behavior. Fix: collect data across deployment conditions, standardize operator instructions, use multi-modal policy architectures (diffusion) for multi-operator data, track and visualize data distribution coverage.

## Interview-ready answer

Dataset curation is arguably the highest-leverage activity in robot learning — more impactful than architecture search or hyperparameter tuning. My curation pipeline: (1) define coverage requirements upfront (task variations, object diversity, approach directions), (2) collect with quality checks in the loop (real-time visualization, automated anomaly detection), (3) post-collection filtering (success verification, kinematic feasibility, statistical outlier removal), (4) balance analysis and targeted augmentation for underrepresented regions, (5) version everything with DVC. Key insight: invest time in understanding your data distribution before training. I always plot action distributions, state-space coverage, and per-task statistics. A common mistake: collecting 1000 demos of the easy case and 10 of the hard case — the policy will only learn the easy case.
