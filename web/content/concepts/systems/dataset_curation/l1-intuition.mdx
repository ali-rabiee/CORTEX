## Intuition

Garbage in, garbage out — but for robots, this is even more critical because bad demonstrations teach bad physical habits. A dataset with 1000 demos where the robot always approaches from the right will produce a policy that can't handle left-side approaches. Curation means ensuring your training data covers the situations the robot will actually encounter, with consistent quality throughout.

## In practice

Curating a bimanual manipulation dataset: (1) Collect 500 teleoperated demonstrations across 10 tasks. (2) Automated filtering: discard episodes where task success is ambiguous, joint velocity exceeds safety limits ($\|\dot{q}\| > \dot{q}_{\max}$), or gripper force is anomalous. (3) Balance check: ensure each task has $\geq 40$ demos, each object orientation bin has $\geq 10$ demos. (4) Quality audit: human reviews a random 10% sample for labeling accuracy. (5) Augmentation: for underrepresented regions, collect targeted demonstrations. (6) Version with DVC: tag as `v1.2`, log statistics (per-task counts, action distribution plots, state-space coverage heatmaps). Result: 420 clean demos that train a better policy than the original 500.
