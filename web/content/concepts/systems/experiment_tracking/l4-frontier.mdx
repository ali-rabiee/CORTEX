## It is a systems problem before it is a discipline problem

<PaperCard refKey="sculley2015debt" />

The framing that survives: the code that trains the model is a small box in a
large diagram, and the surrounding configuration and glue is where the cost is.
Reproducibility inherits that. If the configuration surface is unmanaged, no
amount of individual care produces comparable runs — which is why "be more
careful" has never worked as a remedy and tooling has.

## The evidence

<PaperCard refKey="islam2017repro" />

Reruns of published continuous-control results under different seeds and
implementations, finding that reported orderings often did not survive. This is
what moved the field from suspecting variance was underreported to being able to
point at cases. The now-standard practice of reporting several seeds with a
spread rather than the best run traces back here.

<PaperCard refKey="pineau2021repro" />

The turn from diagnosis to specification: a checklist, a code-submission policy,
a reproducibility challenge — and, unusually, measurements of what each
intervention changed. Reading the checklist as a spec for what a run record must
contain is more useful than reading it as paperwork.

## The result that inverts the instinct

<PaperCard refKey="bouthillier2021variance" />

<Steps>
<Step title="Seeds are not the dominant source">
Data splits, initialisation and hyperparameter tuning contribute comparably or
more. A study varying only the seed is measuring one term of several.
</Step>

<Step title="So fixing things makes intervals too small">
Every factor held constant is removed from the measured spread while remaining
part of the claim. The interval gets tighter and the question it answers gets
narrower, in the same move.
</Step>

<Step title="Randomise what you are generalising over">
If the sentence is "method A beats method B", the split and the initialisation
must vary. The wider interval is the one that matches the sentence.
</Step>

<Step title="Which is not an argument against control">
Hold a factor fixed when you are willing to state the conclusion conditionally —
"on this split, with this backbone". The error is fixing it and then generalising
anyway.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
Together these give the arc: a systems diagnosis in 2015, evidence that results
did not replicate in 2017, a specification in 2021, and then the finding that
even careful practice was systematically understating variance. The last one is
the interesting one to raise in an interview, because it is the step where "be
more rigorous" and "control more variables" come apart — and most people assume
they are the same thing.
</KeyIdea>
