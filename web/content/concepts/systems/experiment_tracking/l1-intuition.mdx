## A run is a measurement, or it is an anecdote

<Intuition>
The point of tracking is not tidiness. It is that a comparison between two runs
supports a causal claim only if exactly one thing differed between them. Code,
config, data version, dependency versions, driver, hardware, seed — every one of
those that can vary without being recorded is an alternative explanation for
whatever you observed. Tracking is the machinery that lets you say "this
difference is attributable to the change I made", and without it you have a
number that looks identical to a real result.
</Intuition>

That framing settles most practical questions. You log a thing if not logging it
would leave an unexplained difference between two runs.

## Why you cannot just reproduce the run

The obvious remedy — set a seed and rerun — does less than people expect.

<Figure src="/figures/nondeterminism-divergence.svg" alt="Weight-space distance between two runs identical except for a 1e-12 perturbation to one weight, on a log scale. In the amplifying regime it doubles every three steps and saturates at the between-seed distance by step 150; in the contracting regime it barely moves over 2000 steps. Both runs reach the same final accuracy." caption="Perturb one weight by one part in a trillion. In an amplifying regime the two runs are as far apart as two different seeds within 150 steps." />

Seeding fixes the pseudorandom draws and nothing else. Many fast GPU kernels
accumulate with atomics, so the reduction order changes between launches; float32
addition is not associative, so the same mathematical sum lands on a different
value in the last bit. Measured on a small MLP in an amplifying regime, that
difference **doubles every three steps**, so a single float32 ulp reaches
between-seed distance in **about 89 steps**.

<KeyIdea>
Two runs can be bitwise different and still be the same experiment. Both runs in
that figure reach identical final accuracy — weight-space reproducibility is lost
long before metric reproducibility is. So the goal is not identical weights, which
is often unattainable, but a *comparable distribution of outcomes*: fix what you
can, record what you cannot, and report over seeds.
</KeyIdea>

## Which regime you are in decides how much this matters

<Steps>
<Step title="Contracting: perturbations decay">
Well-conditioned supervised training at a modest learning rate. A $10^{-12}$
perturbation grew only 2.8× over 2000 steps in the measurement above. Here seeds
genuinely barely matter and people conclude reproducibility is easy.
</Step>

<Step title="Amplifying: perturbations explode">
Aggressive learning rates near the edge of stability — and RL, where the policy
determines its own data, so an early difference changes everything collected
afterwards. Here two runs share nothing but their configuration.
</Step>

<Step title="Both camps are right about their own regime">
This is why the field disagrees about whether seeds matter. The disagreement is
not about rigour; it is about which regime the arguing parties train in.
</Step>

<Step title="The response is the same either way">
You cannot tell from a single run which regime you are in, and the cost of
assuming wrongly is asymmetric — so run multiple seeds and report the spread
regardless.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
The specific trap: a run that looks reproducible because you reran it once and
got a similar number. One repeat cannot distinguish a contracting regime from an
amplifying one that happened to land nearby, and it cannot distinguish a real
effect from seed noise. The comparison that matters is between the size of your
treatment effect and the spread across seeds, which needs several runs of each —
see evaluation methodology for how many.
</Warning>
