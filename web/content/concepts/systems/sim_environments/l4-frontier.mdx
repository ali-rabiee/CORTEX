## The engine the benchmarks were built on

<PaperCard refKey="todorov2012mujoco" />

MuJoCo's contribution was a contact model that is convex and soft enough to solve
quickly and smoothly enough to optimise through. That combination is why a decade
of continuous-control results run on it — and also why those results inherit its
particular softened contact, which is worth remembering when reading transfer
claims.

## Removing the boundary

<PaperGrid>
<PaperCard refKey="makoviychuk2021isaac" />
<PaperCard refKey="freeman2021brax" />
</PaperGrid>

Two routes to the same realisation: the per-step handoff between simulator and
learner is the cost.

<Steps>
<Step title="Isaac Gym: keep everything in device memory">
Physics state, observation assembly and the policy forward pass all on the GPU.
The round trip that needed 24 GB/s at 4096 environments simply stops existing.
</Step>

<Step title="Brax: compile the loop as one program">
Physics in JAX, so the rollout and the update are a single XLA computation. No
per-step dispatch, and the dynamics are differentiable by construction.
</Step>

<Step title="What both give up">
Fidelity, in specific places — simplified contact, restricted model features.
Which is the trade the whole concept is about, made at the level of the engine.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The consequence for the algorithm

<PaperCard refKey="rudin2021minutes" />

The result that makes the point: quadruped locomotion in minutes on a single GPU.
The interesting part is not the wall clock but that the hyperparameters had to
change. Thousands of environments collecting a few dozen steps each is a very
different batch from one environment collecting thousands.

<KeyIdea>
This is the arc worth being able to state. A fast engine enabled a benchmark era;
GPU-resident simulation removed the transfer that capped it; compiling the loop
removed the boundary entirely; and then the algorithm's own settings turned out
to depend on the infrastructure. Throughput was not a separate axis from method —
which is why "we just ran it faster" undersells what changed.
</KeyIdea>
