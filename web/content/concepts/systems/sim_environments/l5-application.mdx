## Failure modes

Sim fidelity mismatch: the simulator's contact model doesn't capture real-world physics for your task. MuJoCo uses soft contact (spring-damper), which diverges from rigid contact in tasks with hard collisions. GPU simulators trade accuracy for speed — parallel environments may have slightly different behavior than serial ones. Symptoms: policy works in sim, fails immediately on real robot. Fix: validate sim behavior against real-robot recordings for your specific task, tune contact parameters to match real force-torque data, use domain randomization to cover sim-real gap.

## Interview-ready answer

Simulator choice depends on the task and scale: Isaac Gym for massively parallel RL training (locomotion, dexterous manipulation), MuJoCo for precise contact-rich tasks and research prototyping, Drake for tasks needing accurate contact mechanics (assembly, insertion). My workflow: prototype in MuJoCo for fast iteration, scale training in Isaac Gym for production, validate in Isaac Sim with realistic rendering for vision policies. Key consideration: always validate your simulator against real-world data for the specific dynamics that matter for your task. No simulator is perfect — but the right sim + domain randomization closes most gaps.
