## Intuition

A 90% success rate in the lab means nothing if you only tested on 10 trials with the same object in the same position. Proper evaluation means: 100+ trials, varied conditions, reporting confidence intervals, and categorizing failure modes. It's the difference between 'it works' and 'it works and I know when it will fail.'

## In practice

Manipulation policy evaluation: 100 trials across 5 object categories $\times$ 4 initial positions = 2000 total trials. Report: overall success rate ($85\% \pm 3\%$), per-category breakdown, failure mode taxonomy (grasp slip: 40% of failures, perception error: 30%, planning failure: 30%). Include sim and real results separately. Compute $p$-value via McNemar's test when comparing two policies on paired trials.
