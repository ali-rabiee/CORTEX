## Failure modes

Cherry-picked results: only reporting best seed, testing only on easy configurations, not controlling for environmental variation. Also: sim evaluation doesn't predict real performance — policies that reach 99% in sim may drop to 60% in real ($\Delta_{\text{sim2real}} = 39\%$). Fix: always report real-robot results, use sufficient trial counts ($N \geq 50$), report standard deviation across seeds, test on held-out configurations.

## Interview-ready answer

Rigorous evaluation is what separates research demos from deployable systems. My evaluation protocol: (1) define success criteria precisely (binary success, not continuous metrics), (2) test across varied conditions (objects, positions, lighting), (3) report mean $\pm$ std across $\geq 3$ seeds and $\geq 50$ trials, (4) categorize every failure. In industry, I also add: edge case discovery (adversarial testing), long-horizon reliability (1000+ sequential executions), and degradation testing (what breaks first as conditions change). The most important metric is rarely the one in the paper — it's the failure mode distribution.
