## Intuition

Vanilla SGD is like navigating by the local slope at each step — noisy and slow in ravines. Momentum is like rolling a ball: it accumulates velocity in consistent gradient directions, damping oscillations in noisy dimensions. Nesterov momentum is even smarter — it peeks ahead to where momentum would take you and corrects based on the gradient there, reducing overshooting. Learning rate schedules are like adjusting your stride: large steps early to cover ground quickly, small steps later for fine-grained convergence.

## In practice

Training ResNet-50 on ImageNet for robot perception pre-training: SGD with momentum $\mu = 0.9$, base learning rate $\alpha = 0.1$, weight decay $5 \times 10^{-4}$, cosine schedule over 90 epochs with linear warmup for 5 epochs. Batch size 256 across 4 GPUs with synchronized SGD. The learning rate warmup prevents early training instability caused by large gradients from random initialization. For fine-tuning on robot data: reduce learning rate by $10\times$ ($\alpha = 0.01$), use shorter cosine schedule over 20 epochs, freeze BatchNorm statistics.
