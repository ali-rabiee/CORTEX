## Failure modes

Failure to account for receptive field limitations: the network cannot reason about relationships between distant image regions if the receptive field is too small. Symptoms: works on cropped close-up images but fails on full scene understanding. Also: translation equivariance breaks at boundaries (padding artifacts) and with strided convolutions (aliasing). Pooling discards precise spatial information needed for pixel-level tasks. Fixes: use dilated convolutions to increase receptive field without losing resolution, feature pyramid networks (FPN) for multi-scale reasoning, and deformable convolutions for adaptive receptive fields.

## Interview-ready answer

CNNs encode translation equivariance through weight sharing and local connectivity — the same filter detects the same pattern everywhere. The hierarchical feature extraction (edges → parts → objects) emerges naturally from stacking convolutional layers. Key design choices: (1) receptive field must cover the relevant spatial context — use dilated convolutions or deeper networks for global reasoning, (2) stride and pooling control the resolution-computation tradeoff, (3) residual connections enable training very deep networks (50-150+ layers). For robotics: CNNs remain the backbone for real-time perception (ResNet, EfficientNet) due to favorable FLOPs vs accuracy compared to ViTs at smaller scales. ViTs surpass CNNs when data and compute are abundant, but CNNs with pre-training remain the practical choice for deployment on edge devices like Jetson.
