## The operator survived; its blind spots got named

Post-2017 work on the Bellman equation falls into three buckets:

**Enrich what it backs up.** Distributional RL propagates the whole return
distribution through the same contraction. It won benchmarks and quietly
became standard inside agents like Rainbow and modern recommender/risk
systems.

**Map where it breaks.** The deadly-triad line of work turned folklore
divergence into measurable conditions, and offline RL (CQL and successors)
patched the specific failure of `max_a` over unsupported actions.

**Challenge its necessity.** Sequence modeling asked whether backups are
needed at all; the answer so far — *needed exactly when you must stitch*.

Interview-ready synthesis: "The Bellman equation is still the only mechanism
we have for *stitching* — combining pieces of different experiences into a
policy better than any of them. Distributional RL upgraded what it
propagates, CQL fenced its optimism, and Decision Transformer showed what
you lose without it. If a 2026 method claims to need no value function, ask
how it stitches."
