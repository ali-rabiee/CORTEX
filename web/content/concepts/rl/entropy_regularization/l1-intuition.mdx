## Intuition

Without entropy regularization, a policy gradient agent will rapidly converge to a deterministic policy — committing to the first decent strategy it finds. The entropy bonus $\beta H(\pi)$ rewards the agent for maintaining uncertainty, like telling a chess player: 'you get bonus points for considering multiple moves.' This prevents premature convergence and maintains exploration throughout training. The coefficient $\beta$ controls the explore-exploit balance: large $\beta$ favors exploration, $\beta \to 0$ recovers the standard (exploit-focused) objective.

## In practice

In PPO for robot manipulation, adding entropy regularization with $\beta = 0.01$. Monitor the policy entropy $H(\pi)$ during training: it should decrease gradually from the initial value (for a Gaussian with $\sigma_0 = 1$ in $d=7$ DOF, $H_0 \approx 9.2$ nats) toward a lower but non-zero value. If $H$ drops below 1.0 too early (before 50% of training), increase $\beta$. In SAC, the dual variable $\alpha$ is optimized to maintain $H(\pi) \geq \bar{H} = -d$, eliminating the need to manually tune $\beta$.
