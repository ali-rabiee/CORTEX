## The problem it exists to solve

Policy gradients are on-policy: the gradient is an expectation over trajectories
from the *current* policy. Take one step and your expensive batch of robot
experience is formally worthless.

That is intolerable when a batch costs an hour of hardware time. So you would
like to take several gradient steps on the same data. The obstacle is that the
further the policy moves, the less the batch describes it — the advantages were
measured under the old policy, and past some point they are describing a
different agent.

<Intuition>
You spent a day watching a driver and noting which decisions worked out. Those
notes are excellent advice for someone who drives roughly like they do. They are
useless advice for someone driving completely differently — the situations they
describe will not come up.

So use the notes, but stop trusting them once you have changed enough that
they no longer describe you.
</Intuition>

## The mechanism

Track how much the policy has moved on each sampled action with the
**probability ratio**

$$
r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)}
$$

$r = 1$ means unchanged; $r = 1.3$ means this action is now 30% more likely.
PPO's objective is built so that once $r$ has moved usefully far, moving further
earns nothing.

<Figure src="/figures/ppo-clip-objective.svg"
        alt="Two plots of the clipped objective against the ratio r. For positive advantage the objective rises then goes flat above 1 plus epsilon. For negative advantage it is flat below 1 minus epsilon and slopes downward above it."
        caption="Flat means no gradient. Note the asymmetry: for a bad action the slope survives on the side that pulls the probability back down, so a policy that has already overshot can still recover." />

<Steps>
<Step title="Collect a batch and freeze its log-probabilities">
Roll out $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$, store states, actions, advantages, and the
log-probability of each action at the time it was taken. That stored number is
the denominator of every ratio from now on.
</Step>

<Step title="Compute the ratio for the current parameters">
$r_t = \exp(\log\pi_\theta - \log\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}})$, using the stored
value. No frozen second network required.
</Step>

<Step title="Take the pessimistic option">
Form both the plain surrogate $r_t \hat A_t$ and the clipped one
$\text{clip}(r_t, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat A_t$, then take the **minimum**.
Taking the min is what makes the objective a lower bound rather than a licence.
</Step>

<Step title="Run several epochs, then discard the batch">
Typically 3-10 epochs of minibatch SGD. Then throw it away and collect again.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
The min is the subtle part. Clipping alone would also switch off the gradient
when the policy had moved too far in a *harmful* direction — exactly when you
most need it back. The min keeps the unclipped term active in that case, so
overshooting on a bad action is recoverable.
</KeyIdea>

## What it is not

<Warning>
PPO does not constrain the ratio. It flattens the objective in a region; it does
not project, clamp, or bound anything about $\theta$. A single large step can
land far outside $[1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]$, and after a few epochs on the same
batch, ratios well outside the range are normal. TRPO enforced a genuine trust
region with a hard KL constraint and second-order machinery; PPO traded that
guarantee for a first-order objective you can implement in five lines. Serious
implementations therefore also log the empirical KL and early-stop the epoch
loop when it exceeds a target — which is, in effect, quietly restoring the thing
that was traded away.
</Warning>

There is a second thing to know about PPO before you trust a benchmark of it.
A careful reproduction found that much of its measured advantage over TRPO came
from code-level details shipped with the reference implementation — advantage
normalisation, value-function clipping, reward scaling, orthogonal
initialisation, learning-rate annealing — rather than from the clipped objective
itself. Level 4 covers that; the implication for you is that "we used PPO" does
not specify an algorithm.
