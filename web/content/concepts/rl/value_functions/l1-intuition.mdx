## Intuition

$V(s)$ is like knowing the overall quality of a chess position. $Q(s,a)$ is knowing the quality of a specific move in that position. The advantage $A(s,a) = Q(s,a) - V(s)$ tells you: 'was this move better or worse than what you'd normally do here?' It's the signal that drives policy improvement — positive $A > 0$ means 'do more of this,' negative $A < 0$ means 'do less.'

## In practice

In actor-critic methods for robot locomotion, the critic learns $V_\phi(s)$ and we estimate $\hat{A}$ using GAE: $$\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)} = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} (\gamma\lambda)^l \delta_{t+l}, \quad \delta_t = r_t + \gamma V_\phi(s_{t+1}) - V_\phi(s_t)$$ Using $\lambda = 0.95$ gives a good bias-variance trade-off. At $\lambda = 1$, GAE reduces to Monte Carlo returns minus baseline; at $\lambda = 0$, it reduces to the one-step TD residual $\delta_t$. The critic architecture typically shares early layers with the actor in simpler tasks.
