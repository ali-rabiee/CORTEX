## Intuition

Sparse rewards are like learning to cook with only a pass/fail test at the end. Reward shaping adds intermediate feedback: 'the sauce smells good' (closer to goal), 'the temperature is right' (subgoal achieved). But bad shaping can teach shortcuts — a robot that hovers near the goal without actually reaching it. The potential-based form $\gamma \Phi(s') - \Phi(s)$ telescopes over a trajectory, leaving only boundary terms, which is why it can't distort the optimal policy.

## In practice

Training a robot to pick and place objects. Sparse reward: $r = +1$ only on successful place. Shaped reward: $r' = r + \Delta d_{\text{goal}} + r_{\text{grasp}} + r_{\text{height}}$. Problem: robot learns to pick up and hold the object (maximizing $r_{\text{grasp}} + r_{\text{height}}$) without placing it. Fix: use potential-based shaping with $\Phi(s) = -\|s_{\text{obj}} - s_{\text{goal}}\|_2$ or curriculum with sparse final reward.
