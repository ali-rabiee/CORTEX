## Formal definition

TRPO maximizes the expected advantage of the new policy over the old, subject to a constraint on how much the policy can change, measured by the KL divergence: $$\max_\theta \; \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \rho_{\theta_{\text{old}}},\, a \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\left[\frac{\pi_\theta(a|s)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a|s)} \hat{A}(s,a)\right] \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbb{E}_s\left[D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot|s) \,\|\, \pi_\theta(\cdot|s)\right)\right] \leq \delta$$ This is solved approximately using a second-order method: the Fisher Information Matrix $F = \mathbb{E}[\nabla \log \pi \, (\nabla \log \pi)^\top]$ and conjugate gradient to compute the natural gradient step $\Delta\theta = F^{-1} g$ where $g = \nabla_\theta L$.
