## Failure modes

Conjugate gradient approximation errors can violate the trust region constraint, causing occasional large updates. Symptoms: sporadic reward drops despite the theoretical monotonic improvement guarantee. Also, TRPO is computationally expensive — each update requires multiple Hessian-vector products $Fv$ via finite differences. For large networks (>10M params), the conjugate gradient solve becomes a bottleneck. Fix: use PPO for a simpler approximation, or reduce CG iterations (10 is standard) with tighter line search.

## Interview-ready answer

TRPO provides a principled trust-region approach to policy optimization by solving $\max_\theta L(\theta)$ subject to $D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\text{old}} \| \pi_\theta) \leq \delta$, with theoretical monotonic improvement guarantees under certain assumptions. It uses the natural gradient $F^{-1}g$ computed via conjugate gradient without ever forming the Fisher matrix $F$ explicitly — just Hessian-vector products. In practice, PPO replaced TRPO because the clipping objective is much simpler to implement, parallelizes better, and performs comparably. However, TRPO's theory is important: it established why constraining KL divergence (not Euclidean distance in parameter space) is the right measure for policy updates. I would use TRPO over PPO only if I needed strict monotonic improvement guarantees.
