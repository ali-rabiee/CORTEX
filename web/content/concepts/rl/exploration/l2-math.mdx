## Formal definition

The exploration-exploitation dilemma is the fundamental tension in sequential decision making: the agent must exploit current knowledge to maximize immediate reward while exploring uncertain actions to improve future decisions. Formally, an optimal exploration strategy minimizes the Bayesian regret: $$\text{BayesRegret}(T) = \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T} V^*(s_t) - Q^*(s_t, a_t)\right]$$ Key approaches include: $\epsilon$-greedy (action $a = \arg\max Q$ with probability $1-\epsilon$, random otherwise), UCB ($a = \arg\max_a [Q(s,a) + c\sqrt{\ln t / N(s,a)}]$), Thompson Sampling (sample $Q \sim P(Q|\mathcal{D})$, act greedily), and intrinsic motivation via curiosity or count-based bonuses: $r^{\text{int}} = \beta / \sqrt{N(s)}$ or $r^{\text{int}} = \|\hat{f}(s_{t+1}) - f_\phi(s_t, a_t)\|^2$ (prediction error).
