## The objective

$$
J(\pi) = \sum_{t} \mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim\rho_\pi}
\Big[r(s_t,a_t) + \alpha\,\mathcal{H}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid s_t)\big)\Big],
\qquad
\mathcal{H}(\pi) = -\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}\big[\log\pi(a\mid s)\big]
$$

Set $\alpha = 0$ and this is ordinary RL, so nothing is lost by adopting it —
maximum entropy is a strict generalisation with a knob.

The value functions inherit the entropy term:

$$
V(s) = \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}\big[Q(s,a) - \alpha\log\pi(a\mid s)\big],
\qquad
Q(s,a) = r(s,a) + \gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{s'}\big[V(s')\big]
$$

<Derivation title="The optimal policy is Boltzmann, and the value is a log-sum-exp">

**Step 1 — the one-state problem.** For a fixed $Q$, find the distribution
maximising $\mathbb{E}_\pi[Q] + \alpha\mathcal{H}(\pi)$ subject to
$\sum_a \pi(a) = 1$. The Lagrangian:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_a \pi(a) Q(a) - \alpha\sum_a \pi(a)\log\pi(a) + \lambda\Big(\sum_a\pi(a) - 1\Big)
$$

**Step 2 — differentiate with respect to $\pi(a)$ and set to zero.**

$$
Q(a) - \alpha\big(\log\pi(a) + 1\big) + \lambda = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\log\pi(a) = \frac{Q(a)}{\alpha} + \frac{\lambda}{\alpha} - 1
$$

**Step 3 — exponentiate and normalise.** The constant is absorbed by the
partition function:

$$
\boxed{\;\pi^*(a\mid s) = \frac{\exp\big(Q(s,a)/\alpha\big)}{Z(s)},
\qquad Z(s) = \int \exp\big(Q(s,a)/\alpha\big)\,da\;}
$$

**Step 4 — substitute back to get the value.** With $\pi = \pi^*$,
$\log\pi^* = Q/\alpha - \log Z$, so
$Q - \alpha\log\pi^* = \alpha\log Z$, a constant in $a$. Therefore

$$
V(s) = \alpha \log \int \exp\big(Q(s,a)/\alpha\big)\, da
$$

a **log-sum-exp**: a smooth maximum, which is where "soft" comes from. Note
$V(s) \ge \max_a Q(s,a)$, and the limits behave: $\alpha\to0$ recovers the hard
max and a greedy policy; $\alpha\to\infty$ gives the uniform policy.

</Derivation>

## What SAC actually computes

The partition function $Z(s)$ is intractable for a continuous action space, so
SAC never evaluates it. Instead the policy improvement step is written as a KL
projection onto the family the network can represent:

$$
\pi_{\text{new}} = \arg\min_{\pi' \in \Pi}\;
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi'(\cdot\mid s)\;\Big\|\;
\frac{\exp\big(Q^{\pi_{\text{old}}}(s,\cdot)/\alpha\big)}{Z(s)}\right)
$$

Expanding the KL gives $\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi'}[\log\pi' - Q/\alpha] + \log Z$,
and $\log Z$ does not depend on $\pi'$ — so it drops out of the gradient. The
actor loss is what remains:

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D},\,a\sim\pi_\theta}
\big[\alpha\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s) - Q(s,a)\big]
$$

<KeyIdea>
The intractable normaliser vanishing from the gradient is what makes the whole
scheme practical. You never need $Z$; you only need to move the policy toward
$\exp(Q/\alpha)$, and the KL objective's gradient does not see the constant.
</KeyIdea>

## The squashing correction

Actions are bounded, so SAC samples $u \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta(s), \sigma_\theta(s))$
and outputs $a = \tanh(u)$. Change of variables then requires the log-Jacobian:

<Derivation title="$\log\pi(a\mid s)$ after the $\tanh$">

For an invertible elementwise map $a = f(u)$, densities transform as
$\pi(a) = \mu(u)\,\big|\det(\mathrm{d}a/\mathrm{d}u)\big|^{-1}$. With
$a_i = \tanh(u_i)$, the Jacobian is diagonal with entries
$\mathrm{d}a_i/\mathrm{d}u_i = 1 - \tanh^2(u_i)$, so

$$
\log\pi(a\mid s) = \log\mu(u\mid s) - \sum_i \log\big(1 - \tanh^2(u_i)\big)
$$

Implementations add a small $\varepsilon$ inside the log, since the term
diverges as $|\tanh u_i| \to 1$.

</Derivation>

<Warning>
Omitting this correction is the most common SAC bug and it does not announce
itself. The term is action-dependent and grows without bound near the action
limits — exactly where a converged policy operates. Skip it and the reported
entropy is too high at the bounds, the auto-tuner drives $\alpha$ too low, and
the policy saturates further in a slow feedback loop. You get a run that trains,
looks fine, and quietly becomes deterministic.
</Warning>

## The temperature as a dual variable

Rather than tuning $\alpha$, pose the constrained problem: maximise expected
reward subject to the policy's entropy being at least $\bar{\mathcal{H}}$.

$$
\max_\pi\; \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_t r_t\Big]
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\mathbb{E}\big[-\log\pi(a_t\mid s_t)\big] \ge \bar{\mathcal{H}}
$$

The Lagrange multiplier on that constraint *is* $\alpha$, and minimising the
dual gives

$$
\mathcal{L}(\alpha) = \mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi}\big[-\alpha\big(\log\pi(a\mid s) + \bar{\mathcal{H}}\big)\big]
$$

Gradient descent on this raises $\alpha$ when entropy is below target and lowers
it when above. The standard heuristic $\bar{\mathcal{H}} = -\dim(\mathcal{A})$
is exactly that — a heuristic — but it removed the one hyperparameter that
otherwise had to be retuned for every reward scale.

## Two stabilisers, each for a named failure

<Steps>
<Step title="Twin critics with a min, against overestimation">
The soft value still contains a maximisation, so the Jensen argument applies:
the expectation of a max over noisy estimates exceeds the max of the
expectations. Using $\min(Q_1, Q_2)$ in the target trades a downward bias for
the upward one, and bootstrapping means the upward bias would otherwise
compound.
</Step>

<Step title="Target networks, against the triad">
Off-policy data plus bootstrapping plus function approximation has no
convergence guarantee. A slowly-updated target copy (Polyak $\tau \approx 0.005$)
slows the feedback loop between the estimate and the thing it is being fitted
to. It does not remove the problem; it makes the timescales separate.
</Step>

<Step title="Entropy in *both* the critic target and the actor loss">
The critic target subtracts $\alpha\log\pi(a'\mid s')$ so that it estimates the
*soft* $Q$. Keep the entropy in the actor and drop it from the target and the
two components are optimising different reward functions — a subtle
inconsistency that shows up as a policy fighting its own critic.
</Step>
</Steps>
