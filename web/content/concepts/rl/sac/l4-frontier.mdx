## From algorithm to infrastructure

SAC's 2018 contribution was consolidation: maximum-entropy exploration,
clipped double-Q, and pathwise gradients in one package that *didn't need
retuning per task*. The decade since has been about making it cheap enough
for hardware:

  <PaperCard refKey="smith2022walk" />

The sample-efficiency line (REDQ → DroQ → RLPD) all attacks the same
constraint: gradient steps are nearly free, environment steps on a robot are
not. Controlling Q-estimation bias at high update-to-data ratios is what
unlocked minutes-scale legged learning and hours-scale manipulation.

## Where it sits in the VLA era

<PaperGrid>
  <PaperCard refKey="luo2024hilserl" />
</PaperGrid>

Imitation-first pretraining (ACT, Diffusion Policy, π0-class VLAs) owns
manipulation breadth, but it plateaus at demonstrator quality. The emerging
division of labor: **BC/VLA for the prior, SAC-lineage off-policy RL for
surpassing it** — online, on hardware, with humans correcting failures.

<KeyIdea>

Interview-ready synthesis: "SAC is the diesel engine of continuous control:
unglamorous, everywhere, hard to kill. Its modern relevance isn't beating
diffusion policies at imitation — it's that every serious 'RL on real
robots' result of the last few years (20-minute walking, HIL-SERL's
near-perfect manipulation) is SAC machinery with better bias control at
high update ratios."

</KeyIdea>
