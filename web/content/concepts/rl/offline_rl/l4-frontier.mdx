## A field organized by one design decision

Every offline RL method answers: *how do you improve on the dataset without
trusting Q where the dataset is silent?*

- **Constrain the policy** to stay near behavior (BCQ, TD3+BC) — simple,
  caps improvement.
- **Penalize the values** on OOD actions (CQL) — strong offline, overly
  pessimistic for fine-tuning until Cal-QL.
- **Stay in-sample** (IQL) — never query OOD; the robotics favorite for its
  stability.

## The 2023-2026 turn: offline as pretraining, not destination

Pure offline benchmarks saturated; the action moved to **offline-to-online**
(pretrain on logs, fine-tune on hardware without a dip) and to **scale**
(can Bellman methods ride the same data flywheel as VLAs?).

Against the backdrop of imitation-based robot foundation models, offline
RL's enduring claim is narrow but decisive: **it is the only way to convert
failure logs into policy improvement** — and fleets produce failures for
free.

<KeyIdea>

Interview-ready synthesis: "Classify any offline RL method by where it gets
its max — constrained, penalized, or in-sample — and you can predict its
behavior. The modern stack uses offline RL less as a standalone solution
and more as the value-aware layer of a pipeline: BC/VLA prior → offline RL
on mixed logs → calibrated online fine-tuning (Cal-QL/RLPD) on the robot."

</KeyIdea>
