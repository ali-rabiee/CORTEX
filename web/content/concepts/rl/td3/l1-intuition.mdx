## Intuition

DDPG was the first continuous-action DQN-style algorithm but suffered from severe overestimation and brittle training. TD3 fixes this with three simple ideas: (1) take the minimum of two critics — like getting a second opinion to avoid being overconfident; (2) don't update the actor until the critics agree — let the critics settle before making policy decisions; (3) add noise to the target action — this smooths out sharp Q-peaks that the policy might exploit. Together, these create a much more conservative and stable learning process. SAC later improved on TD3 by adding entropy regularization for automatic exploration.

## In practice

TD3 for a simulated Franka Panda reaching task: deterministic policy $\pi_\phi: \mathbb{R}^{14} \to \mathbb{R}^7$ (proprioception to joint velocity targets). Two critics $Q_{\theta_1}, Q_{\theta_2}$: each takes $(s, a)$ and outputs scalar value. Exploration: add Gaussian noise $a = \pi_\phi(s) + \epsilon, \; \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.1)$ during data collection. Target smoothing: $\sigma = 0.2$, clip $c = 0.5$. Delayed updates: $d = 2$ (critic updates twice per actor update). Hyperparameters: $|\mathcal{B}| = 10^6$, $B = 256$, $\tau = 0.005$, actor/critic LR $= 3 \times 10^{-4}$. TD3 matches SAC performance on many MuJoCo tasks but without the entropy tuning complexity.
