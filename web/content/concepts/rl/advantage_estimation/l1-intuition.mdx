## Intuition

Imagine estimating how good a chess move was. You could look one step ahead (low variance but biased by your imperfect position evaluator — that's $\lambda = 0$, the TD residual $\delta_t$). Or you could play out the entire game and use the actual outcome (unbiased but high variance — that's $\lambda = 1$, Monte Carlo). GAE with $\lambda = 0.95$ is like mostly trusting the game outcome but slightly anchoring to your position evaluator at each step. The $(\gamma\lambda)^l$ weighting means distant TD residuals contribute exponentially less.

## In practice

In a PPO implementation for quadruped locomotion: collect rollouts of length $T=2048$, compute TD residuals $\delta_t = r_t + \gamma V_\phi(s_{t+1}) - V_\phi(s_t)$, then compute GAE advantages backwards: $\hat{A}_T = 0$, $\hat{A}_t = \delta_t + \gamma\lambda \hat{A}_{t+1}$. With $\gamma = 0.99$, $\lambda = 0.95$: the effective lookahead is $\frac{1}{1-\gamma\lambda} \approx 20$ steps. Normalize: $\hat{A} \leftarrow (\hat{A} - \mu)/\sigma$ per batch. Value targets: $V_{\text{target},t} = \hat{A}_t + V_\phi(s_t)$.
