## Intuition

State is 'ground truth' — the full physics of the world. Observation is what your sensors actually give you. A robot arm's true state includes all joint angles, velocities, and the exact pose of every object. But its observation might be a single RGB camera image — it can't see behind objects, can't directly measure velocities, and has noise. The gap between state and observation is the core challenge of real-world RL. In simulation, you often have access to the full state (privileged information); in the real world, you almost never do.

## In practice

A manipulation task with a Franka Panda robot: full state $s = (q \in \mathbb{R}^7, \dot{q} \in \mathbb{R}^7, x_{\text{obj}} \in SE(3), v_{\text{obj}} \in \mathbb{R}^6, f_{\text{contact}} \in \mathbb{R}^6)$ — 33 dimensions. Observation options: (1) proprioception only: $(q, \dot{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^{14}$ — can't see objects; (2) proprioception + object pose from perception: $\mathbb{R}^{14+7}$ — noisy pose estimates; (3) raw images: $o \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ — partial observability. Common approach: train with privileged state in sim, distill to observation-based policy. Frame stacking ($k=3$ frames) helps recover velocity information from position-only observations.
