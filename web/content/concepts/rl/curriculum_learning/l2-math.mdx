## Formal definition

Curriculum learning presents training tasks in a structured order of increasing difficulty to improve learning efficiency and final performance. Formally, a curriculum is a sequence of task distributions $\{\mathcal{T}_1, \mathcal{T}_2, \ldots, \mathcal{T}_K\}$ where the agent trains on $\mathcal{T}_k$ before $\mathcal{T}_{k+1}$. Automatic curriculum methods adapt the task distribution based on the agent's competence: (1) self-paced learning: $\min_\theta \min_{v \in [0,1]^N} \sum_i v_i L_i(\theta) - \lambda \sum_i v_i$ (weight down hard tasks); (2) goal-conditioned curricula: sample goals from the frontier of competence; (3) domain randomization as implicit curriculum: $p(\text{env params}) \to \text{wider}$ over training.
