## Failure modes

High variance gradients causing unstable training. The variance of the estimator $\nabla \log \pi \cdot \hat{A}$ can be enormous. Symptoms: erratic reward curves, no convergence even with enough data. Fix: use advantage estimation (GAE with $\lambda = 0.95$), increase batch size $N$, add baseline $V_\phi(s)$. Also: for continuous actions, unconstrained $\sigma$ can collapse to zero (similar to PPO entropy collapse).

## Interview-ready answer

Policy gradients are the foundation of modern RL for continuous control. The key equation is the policy gradient theorem: $\nabla J = \mathbb{E}[\nabla \log \pi_\theta \cdot \hat{A}]$. In practice, we use GAE with $\lambda = 0.95$ for advantage estimation and normalize advantages per batch: $\hat{A} \leftarrow (\hat{A} - \mu) / \sigma$. The main challenge is high variance — addressed by baselines $V_\phi(s)$, larger batches, and methods like PPO/TRPO that constrain update size. Policy gradients naturally handle continuous action spaces $\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, unlike DQN which requires discretization.
