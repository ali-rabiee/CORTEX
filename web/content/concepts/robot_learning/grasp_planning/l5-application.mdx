## Failure modes

Grasp prediction fails on transparent/reflective objects (depth sensor returns noisy/missing data), thin objects (insufficient contact surface), and novel geometries outside the training distribution. Also: predicted grasp may be kinematically unreachable for the robot arm. Symptoms: gripper closes on air, object slips during lift, or arm contorts into singular configuration. Fix: RGB-D fusion for transparent objects, tactile feedback for slip detection, IK-aware grasp filtering, and domain-specific fine-tuning.

## Interview-ready answer

Grasp planning has shifted from analytical (force closure, form closure) to learned methods. Modern pipelines: point cloud $\rightarrow$ neural network $\rightarrow$ ranked 6-DOF grasp proposals $\rightarrow$ IK filter $\rightarrow$ execute. Contact-GraspNet and AnyGrasp are state-of-the-art, predicting diverse grasps from partial point clouds with >90% success. The key engineering decisions: (1) sensor choice (depth quality matters enormously), (2) grasp filtering (reachability, collision checking), (3) fallback strategies (re-plan if first grasp fails). For production, I combine learned grasp prediction with classical collision checking and force-based grasp success detection.
