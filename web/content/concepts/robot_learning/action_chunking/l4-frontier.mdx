## The paper that named it

<PaperCard refKey="zhao2023act" />

ACT's contribution is usually remembered as the hardware — a $20k bimanual
teleoperation rig — but the algorithmic core is chunking plus temporal
ensembling. The ablations are the part worth reading: they separate the CVAE,
the transformer, and the chunking, and chunking carries most of the gain.

## Convergent evolution

<PaperCard refKey="chi2023diffusion" />

Diffusion Policy arrives at sequence prediction from the opposite direction. Its
goal is representing multi-modal $p(a \mid o)$; denoising naturally operates over
a whole action-sequence tensor, so it chunks by construction.

<KeyIdea>
Two 2023 papers with different motivations — one attacking decision points, one
attacking mode averaging — both landed on "predict a sequence." That convergence
is the strongest evidence that single-step action prediction was the wrong
default all along.
</KeyIdea>

## It survives changes of data source

<PaperCard refKey="chi2024umi" />

UMI collects demonstrations with a handheld gripper and no robot at all, then
trains chunked diffusion policies on the result. The recipe holding up under a
completely different collection device is a good sign it's capturing something
structural about the control problem rather than an artefact of teleoperation.

## Why it became standard

<Steps>
<Step title="It costs nothing">
No extra demonstrations, no online expert, no new objective. A wider output
layer and a different dataset slice.
</Step>

<Step title="It composes with everything">
Chunking is orthogonal to the action representation. Diffusion, discretised
tokens, and Gaussian heads all chunk. Nothing has to be given up to adopt it.
</Step>

<Step title="It makes large policies deployable">
For a VLA, one forward pass per chunk decouples control rate from model size.
Without chunking, billion-parameter policies simply could not run fast enough to
close a control loop.
</Step>
</Steps>
