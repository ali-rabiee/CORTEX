## Intuition

Two ways to learn from watching a chef: (1) mimic their exact hand movements at each step (behavior cloning), or (2) figure out what they're optimizing for — flavor, presentation, efficiency — and then optimize for that yourself (inverse RL). The first is simpler but brittle; the second transfers better because you understand the 'why' behind the actions.

## In practice

Comparison on a contact-rich peg-in-hole task with 100 human demonstrations: BC achieves 60% success, DAgger reaches 90% with 3 rounds of corrections, GAIL reaches 80% but needs $10^6$ environment steps for the RL inner loop, IRL (MaxEntIRL) recovers a reward that enables 85% success and generalizes to new peg shapes unseen in demos. In practice, BC + action chunking often matches or beats IRL/GAIL with far less compute, which is why diffusion-policy-style BC has become dominant.
