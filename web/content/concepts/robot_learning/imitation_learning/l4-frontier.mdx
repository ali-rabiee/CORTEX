## The spine

<PaperCard refKey="ross2011dagger" />

Everything after this either accepts the $O(\epsilon T^2)$ diagnosis and pays
for online labels, or accepts it and attacks a different term.

## Manufacturing the expert

<PaperCard refKey="levine2016visuomotor" />

The insight that outlived the specific algorithm: in simulation you can *build*
the queryable expert DAgger assumes, out of privileged state. Most sim2real
pipelines are descendants.

## Where real-robot imitation actually landed

<PaperGrid>
<PaperCard refKey="zhao2023act" />
<PaperCard refKey="chi2023diffusion" />
</PaperGrid>

Both keep a fixed offline dataset and no online expert. ACT cuts the number of
decision points; Diffusion Policy fixes the action distribution. Together they
reset expectations about what plain cloning can achieve.

## The current bottleneck

<PaperCard refKey="ebert2021bridge" />

<KeyIdea>
The narrative that reads as current: the algorithm question is largely settled —
chunked, expressive, cloned policies work. The open question is **data**: how to
collect it cheaply, how to pool it across robots with different bodies, and how
to compose datasets so policies generalise. When an interviewer asks what you'd
work on next, that is where the field's attention actually is.
</KeyIdea>
