## The origin

<PaperCard refKey="ross2011dagger" />

Worth reading properly rather than citing from memory. The reduction to online
learning is what lets the bound be *inherited* from no-regret theory instead of
argued from scratch, and that framing is the reason the paper still gets cited
fifteen years later.

## Where the idea actually won

<PaperCard refKey="levine2016visuomotor" />

DAgger's most durable legacy is not the algorithm — it's the observation that a
**queryable expert is worth manufacturing**. In simulation you can build one for
free from privileged state: exact object poses, contact forces, everything the
real robot cannot see.

<Steps>
<Step title="Solve the easy problem with full state">
A trajectory optimiser, motion planner, or RL policy with access to ground-truth
state solves the task. This is far easier than solving it from pixels.
</Step>

<Step title="Distil into the observation the robot really has">
Roll out the student, and let the privileged expert label the states it reaches.
Labels cost nothing, so DAgger's binding constraint evaporates.
</Step>

<Step title="Ship the student">
Only the student runs on hardware. The expert was scaffolding.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
"Privileged expert distillation" is the phrase to have ready. It reframes DAgger
from a technique with an impractical prerequisite into a technique whose
prerequisite you *construct* — and it's how a large fraction of sim2real
locomotion and manipulation pipelines are actually built.
</KeyIdea>

## What replaced it for real-robot imitation

<PaperCard refKey="zhao2023act" />

ACT gets fine bimanual manipulation from roughly fifty fixed demonstrations,
never querying an expert online. That is the practical counter-argument: rather
than buying labels on the learner's distribution, shrink the number of places
the learner can go wrong.

Both routes attack $O(\epsilon T^2)$. DAgger attacks $\epsilon$ by fixing the
distribution it's measured under; chunking attacks $T$ by cutting the number of
decision points. On real hardware with human demonstrators, the second has
simply been cheaper.
