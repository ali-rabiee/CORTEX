## Failure modes

Planning in narrow passages (e.g., reaching into a tight shelf): RRT has low probability of sampling in the narrow $\mathcal{C}_{\text{free}}$ passage, causing timeout. Also: plans may be jerky or unnecessarily long without post-processing. Symptoms: planner takes >10s or fails entirely, robot takes a circuitous path. Fix: use bidirectional RRT-Connect, add task-space biasing, shortcut smoothing, or switch to optimization-based methods (TrajOpt) for constrained scenarios.

## Interview-ready answer

Motion planning is the bridge between high-level policy outputs and safe robot execution. For manipulation, I typically use: RRT-Connect for general planning (fast, probabilistically complete), TrajOpt for constrained tasks (maintains end-effector orientation during pouring), and pre-computed roadmaps for repetitive tasks. In practice, the biggest challenge is not the planning algorithm but the collision model — stale or inaccurate scene geometry causes collisions. My pipeline: perception $\rightarrow$ scene update $\rightarrow$ plan $\rightarrow$ validate $\rightarrow$ execute, with real-time collision checking during execution. For learned policies, motion planning serves as a safety layer between the policy's desired actions and actual execution.
