## The object being defined

A visuomotor policy factors into an encoder and a head,

$$
\pi_\theta(a \mid o) = g_\psi\big(f_\phi(I), \; q\big),
\qquad
f_\phi: \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3} \to \mathbb{R}^{d},
\quad
g_\psi: \mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_q} \to \mathcal{A}
$$

with $I$ the image (or several), $q$ proprioception, and $\theta = (\phi, \psi)$.
Every architecture in this literature is a choice of $f_\phi$, $g_\psi$, and
which of them receives gradient.

<KeyIdea>
"End-to-end" is not a vibe, it is a statement about a derivative:
$\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\phi \neq 0$. The control loss reaches back into
the visual features and reshapes them. Freeze $\phi$ and you no longer have an
end-to-end system — you have a fixed feature extractor with a learned
controller, which is a different object with different failure modes.
</KeyIdea>

## Spatial softmax

The pooling choice is where most of the architectural signal lives. Let
$f_{cij}$ be the pre-pooling activation of channel $c$ at pixel $(i,j)$, and let
$(x_{ij}, y_{ij})$ be that pixel's normalised image coordinates.

<Derivation title="From a feature map to a differentiable keypoint">

**Step 1 — normalise each channel over space, not over channels.**

$$
s_{cij} = \frac{\exp(f_{cij}/\alpha)}{\sum_{i'j'} \exp(f_{ci'j'}/\alpha)}
$$

Each channel now carries a probability distribution over pixel locations:
*where* is this feature.

**Step 2 — take the expectation.**

$$
(x_c, y_c) = \sum_{ij} s_{cij}\,(x_{ij},\, y_{ij})
$$

A $C$-channel map becomes $2C$ numbers, and every one of them is a coordinate.

**Step 3 — check the limit.** As $\alpha \to 0$ the softmax concentrates on the
maximal activation and $(x_c,y_c) \to \arg\max_{ij} f_{cij}$. So this is a soft
argmax with a temperature: a keypoint detector you can backpropagate through.

**The assumption doing the work is unimodality.** The expectation is a faithful
summary of the map only when the map has one peak. Two equally strong peaks —
two identical objects, or an object and its reflection — return the midpoint, a
location with nothing at it. No downstream head can undo that, because the
information left the network at this layer.

</Derivation>

Contrast with global average pooling, $\bar f_c = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{ij} f_{cij}$,
which answers "is this feature present?" and deliberately discards where. That
translation invariance is the entire point in classification and is a liability
in control, where the action is largely a function of position.

## What augmentation is actually asserting

Random cropping is usually filed under regularisation, but it states a prior.
Training on shifted crops with unchanged action labels demands

$$
g_\psi\big(f_\phi(\mathrm{shift}_\delta(I))\big) \approx g_\psi\big(f_\phi(I)\big)
\qquad \text{for small } \|\delta\|
$$

— the action must not depend on a few-pixel translation of the frame. For a
fixed camera whose mounting is repeatable only to within a few pixels, that is
exactly right, and it forbids the encoder from keying on absolute pixel
positions of background clutter.

<Warning>
The prior is a statement about *small* $\delta$, and it is false for large ones:
if the camera moves by 30 pixels, the correct action genuinely changes. Crop
augmentation that is too aggressive teaches an invariance the task does not
have, and precision suffers. Colour jitter is safer because illumination really
is a nuisance variable, and there is no scale at which it stops being one.
</Warning>

## The observation is not the state

Formally the control problem is a POMDP, not an MDP: the optimal action depends
on latent variables $s$ that a single frame does not determine.

<Steps>
<Step title="Velocity">
One frame fixes positions and says nothing about their derivatives. Two frames
recover velocity by finite difference, which is why a two-frame history is the
common default rather than a real memory.
</Step>

<Step title="Contact and force">
Whether the fingers have closed on the object is frequently invisible at camera
resolution, and it changes the correct action completely. Proprioception,
gripper width, and force-torque readings are cheap and exact — concatenate them.
</Step>

<Step title="Anything occluded">
The contents of a drawer, or an object behind the arm. No amount of frame
stacking fixes this; it requires memory over the episode, which is one of the
arguments for transformer policies over frame-stacked conv nets.
</Step>
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
Two failure sources worth keeping apart, because they are fixed differently.
**Representation loss** — spatial softmax averaging two peaks, pooling
discarding position, a pretrained encoder invariant to what you needed — is an
encoder problem. **Partial observability** is an input problem, fixed by giving
the policy more to look at, not by a better encoder.
</KeyIdea>
