## Failure modes

Visual overfitting: policy memorizes specific visual features (table texture, lighting) rather than task-relevant structure. The encoder $f_\phi$ fits spurious correlations in the training visuals. Symptoms: works in training environment, fails with any visual change. Fix: aggressive data augmentation (random crops, color jitter, background randomization), use pre-trained frozen vision encoders (R3M, CLIP, DINOv2) so $f_\phi$ is already robust.

## Interview-ready answer

Visuomotor policies $\pi_\theta(a | o_{\text{img}})$ are the standard approach for learning manipulation from images. The key architectural choices: use a pre-trained vision encoder $f_\phi$ (DINOv2 or R3M) rather than training from scratch, add spatial features via spatial softmax (not just global pooling), and apply heavy data augmentation. For sample efficiency, pre-trained encoders are a game-changer — they reduce the demo requirement from thousands to tens. The main risk is visual overfitting, which is addressed by augmentation and domain randomization. In practice, I prefer frozen pre-trained encoders with a lightweight policy head $g_\psi$ for most manipulation tasks.
