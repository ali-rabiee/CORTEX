## Failure modes

Action space mismatch: human controller has different kinematics than the robot, causing unnatural demonstrations (e.g., VR hand tracking mapped to a parallel-jaw gripper). High latency in the teleoperation loop causes the operator to overshoot, producing jerky trajectories. Operator fatigue over long collection sessions degrades demonstration quality. Symptoms: high variance in demo quality, some demos have sudden jerky motions, policy learns erratic behavior. Fix: match control interfaces to robot morphology (ALOHA approach), minimize loop latency, limit session duration, filter/review demos post-collection.

## Interview-ready answer

Teleoperation quality directly determines imitation learning success. My hierarchy of teleoperation methods: (1) kinematic mirroring (ALOHA-style leader-follower, best for bimanual), (2) VR controllers (good for single-arm, flexible), (3) kinesthetic teaching (good for short motions, intuitive), (4) keyboard/spacemouse (last resort, limited dexterity). Key engineering considerations: action space mapping (Cartesian vs joint), control latency (\<20ms target), recording frequency (50Hz typical), and operator training. I always do a data quality audit after collection: visualize action distributions, check for outliers, and discard demos with anomalous kinematics.
