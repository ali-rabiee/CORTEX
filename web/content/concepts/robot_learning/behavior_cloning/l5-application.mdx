## Failure modes

Covariate shift / compounding errors: the policy makes a small error $\epsilon$ at each step, entering states where $d_{\pi_\theta}(s) \neq d_{\pi^*}(s)$, causing the total error to grow as $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon T^2)$ over horizon $T$. Symptoms: works in easy cases but fails on slight perturbations, degrades over longer horizons. Fix: use DAgger (online expert correction), add noise during data collection, use action chunking, or switch to offline RL.

## Interview-ready answer

BC is the simplest imitation learning approach — supervised learning minimizing $\|\pi_\theta(s) - a^*\|^2$ on expert $(s, a)$ pairs. It works surprisingly well when you have enough diverse demonstrations and the right architecture. The fundamental limitation is compounding errors from covariate shift: the learned state distribution $d_{\pi_\theta}$ diverges from the expert distribution $d_{\pi^*}$, with error growing as $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$. Practical fixes: DAgger for online correction, action chunking (predicting sequences of $K$ actions rather than single actions), data augmentation, and collecting demonstrations with intentional perturbations. For industry, BC is often the first thing to try — if it works with 50-100 demos, you've saved months over RL.
