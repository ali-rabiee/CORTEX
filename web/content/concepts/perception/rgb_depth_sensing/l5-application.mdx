## Failure modes

Structured light interference: multiple RealSense cameras facing each other produce corrupted depth from overlapping IR patterns. ToF multipath: light bouncing off multiple surfaces before returning creates ghost depth readings, especially in corners and concave geometry. Stereo failure on textureless surfaces: smooth white tables produce no disparity matches $\rightarrow$ depth holes. Symptoms: flickering depth values, systematic depth bias, holes in depth map. Fix: for multi-camera structured light, use hardware sync with alternating IR projector duty cycles; for ToF multipath, use multi-frequency modulation; for textureless stereo, project auxiliary texture patterns.

## Interview-ready answer

Sensor selection is the first design decision in any perception pipeline. For indoor manipulation at $< 1.5\,\text{m}$, structured light (RealSense D435) offers the best price/performance ratio — sub-cm accuracy, good resolution, easy ROS integration. For larger workspaces or outdoor, stereo (ZED 2) or ToF (Azure Kinect) is better. The key engineering considerations: (1) operating range vs accuracy tradeoff, (2) failure modes — structured light fails on transparent/specular surfaces and in sunlight, ToF has multipath artifacts, stereo needs texture; (3) multi-camera setups need interference management; (4) always apply temporal and spatial filtering to raw depth. I typically fuse active depth with monocular depth estimation for robustness.
