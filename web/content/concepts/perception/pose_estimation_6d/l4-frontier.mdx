## The decomposition that stuck

<PaperCard refKey="xiang2018posecnn" />

Three tasks rather than one: segment the object, estimate translation by having
each pixel vote for a direction to the centre, and regress rotation separately.
The voting is a robustness choice — occlusion removes votes instead of corrupting
a single global estimate — and the decomposition is what most later systems
inherit.

PoseCNN also introduced a symmetry-aware loss, which is the practical
acknowledgement that the target is not unique.

<PaperCard refKey="wang2019densefusion" />

The fusion argument: RGB tells you what and where in the image, depth tells you
the geometry, and combining them late throws away the chance for each to
disambiguate the other locally. Per-pixel fusion lets a depth discontinuity and a
colour edge inform the same feature.

## The output space is a constraint

<PaperCard refKey="zhou2019continuity" />

The paper that reframes a widespread practice as a mistake, and does so with a
topological argument rather than a benchmark.

<Steps>
<Step title="$SO(3)$ is not simply connected">
Its fundamental group is $\mathbb{Z}_2$ — a $2\pi$ rotation is a loop that cannot
be contracted, and lifting it to the quaternion double cover ends at $-q$.
</Step>

<Step title="So no continuous section exists">
Any single-valued quaternion output must jump somewhere, and a network is
continuous.
</Step>

<Step title="Below five dimensions, this is unavoidable">
Not a quaternion quirk. Euler angles and axis-angle have their own cuts.
</Step>

<Step title="Six dimensions with Gram–Schmidt is the practical fix">
Measured in a controlled comparison: 0.44° mean and 1.27° worst case, against
4.74° and 178.66° for quaternions.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Off per-object training

<PaperCard refKey="wen2024foundationpose" />

<KeyIdea>
The economically decisive change is not accuracy but the cost of adding an
object. Per-object models meant a warehouse with a changing catalogue, or a
small-batch line, could not use pose estimation at all — each SKU was a training
project. Generalising from a CAD model or a few reference images makes it a
configuration step. That is the same pattern as elsewhere in robot learning:
the contribution that moves a method into production is often the one that
removes a per-instance setup cost rather than the one that improves a benchmark.
</KeyIdea>
