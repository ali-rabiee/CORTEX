## The projection, and where the coupling lives

For a point $P$ on a plane at distance $Z$, the pinhole model gives

$$
u = f_x \frac{X}{Z} + c_x, \qquad v = f_y \frac{Y}{Z} + c_y
$$

<Derivation title="A frontal plane cannot separate $f$ from $Z$">

Take the board fronto-parallel, so every point shares the same depth $Z$. A board
point at planar coordinate $(X, Y)$ projects to

$$
u = \frac{f_x}{Z}X + c_x
$$

The image depends on $f_x$ and $Z$ **only through the ratio** $f_x/Z$. Replacing
$(f_x, Z)$ with $(\lambda f_x, \lambda Z)$ leaves every pixel identical, so the
data cannot distinguish them.

Now try to fix it with more views. View $i$ at distance $Z_i$ contributes one
measurable quantity, the image scale $s_i = f_x/Z_i$, and one new unknown $Z_i$.
With $N$ views:

$$
\text{measurements} = N, \qquad \text{unknowns} = \underbrace{N}_{Z_i} + \underbrace{1}_{f_x}
$$

Always one short. **The assumption doing the work is that the board is planar and
frontal**, which makes the per-view projection a pure similarity. Tilt the board
and a point's depth becomes $Z + \Delta Z(X,Y)$, so the near edge projects larger
than the far edge by an amount depending on $f_x$ and $Z$ differently — and the
degeneracy is gone.

</Derivation>

Measured, calibrating a camera with true $f_x = 800$ from ten views:

| protocol | RMS reproj | $f_x$ | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fronto-parallel, fixed $Z$ | 0.184 px | 6000 | +650% |
| fronto-parallel, varied $Z$ | 0.184 px | 4365 | +446% |
| tilt 10° | 0.192 px | 809 | +1.1% |
| tilt 35° | 0.190 px | 799 | −0.1% |

<KeyIdea>
Two different initialisations, $f_0 = 700$ and $f_0 = 600$, converged to 6000.1
and 6000.0. Identical to four significant figures from different starting points
is the signature of a genuine flat direction rather than a stuck optimiser — the
residual is insensitive along it, so the returned value is decided by whatever
weakly breaks the tie, here small lateral translations and pixel noise. This is
the same unobservability argument as monocular metric depth, and worth
recognising as one idea in two places.
</KeyIdea>

## Error propagation

For an object of known size $W$ spanning $w$ pixels, $Z = fW/w$, so

$$
\frac{\delta Z}{Z} = \frac{\delta f}{f}
$$

Focal error transfers into depth error one-for-one. The 446% figure above is
27 cm of depth error at a 60 cm working distance. And for detection noise:

$$
\delta X = \frac{Z}{f}\,\delta u
$$

At $f = 800$ px, one pixel is 0.75 mm at 60 cm and 6.25 mm at 5 m — **linear** in
$Z$, in contrast with stereo depth error, which grows quadratically.

## Hand-eye observability

The hand-eye transform $X$ satisfies $AX = XB$ for corresponding robot and camera
motions. Taking rotations:

$$
R_A R_X = R_X R_B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_A = R_X R_B R_X^{\top}
$$

<Derivation title="Why one motion is not enough">

Conjugation by $R_X$ preserves rotation angle and maps axis to axis: if $R_B$ has
axis $\hat{b}$, then $R_X R_B R_X^\top$ has axis $R_X \hat{b}$. So the constraint
says exactly

$$
R_X \hat{b} = \hat{a}
$$

which fixes where one unit vector goes — two constraints on three rotational
degrees of freedom. **Rotation about $\hat{a}$ itself is unconstrained**, leaving
a one-parameter family.

A second motion with axis $\hat{b}_2 \neq \pm\hat{b}_1$ adds
$R_X \hat{b}_2 = \hat{a}_2$, which removes the remaining freedom. If the axes are
parallel the second constraint duplicates the first and the family survives.

</Derivation>

Observability is not binary in practice — it degrades continuously as the axes
approach parallel. Measured with 0.15° of noise on the observed camera motions:

| angle between motion axes | recovered $R_X$ error |
| --- | --- |
| 1° | **15.96°** |
| 5° | 3.02° |
| 15° | 1.09° |
| 45° | 0.46° |
| 90° | 0.41° |

<Warning>
Nothing in this fails loudly. At 5° separation the system is formally determined,
the solver returns a rotation, and it is 3° wrong — which at a 50 cm working
distance is about 2.6 cm of position error, applied consistently. The practical
rule that follows is to rotate about genuinely different axes during collection,
and the table says 45° is already as good as 90°, so this costs nothing but
intent.
</Warning>
