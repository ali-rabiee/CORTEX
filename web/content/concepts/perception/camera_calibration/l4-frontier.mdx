## Hand-eye, and why the rule is about axes

<PaperGrid>
<PaperCard refKey="tsai1989handeye" />
<PaperCard refKey="park1994axxb" />
</PaperGrid>

Tsai and Lenz's structural move is to solve rotation first, from the motion axes
alone, and then translation linearly given that rotation. That decomposition is
why the practical guidance has always been phrased in terms of rotation axes:
they are literally what the first stage consumes.

Park and Martin then recast the problem on the Euclidean group, which is where
the condition stops being a rule of thumb. Conjugation preserves angle and maps
axis to axis, so $R_A = R_X R_B R_X^\top$ says exactly $R_X\hat{b} = \hat{a}$ —
two constraints, three degrees of freedom, one motion short.

## Making it something anyone can do

<PaperCard refKey="zhang2000flexible" />

Before this, calibration meant a machined 3D target with known point positions.
Zhang showed a printed plane at several *unknown* orientations is enough, which
is the reason every toolbox works the way it does.

<Steps>
<Step title="A plane gives less constraint than a 3D rig">
So the missing information has to come from somewhere else.
</Step>

<Step title="It comes from varying the orientation">
Which is supplied by the user, implicitly, in how they move the board.
</Step>

<Step title="So the failure mode moved from equipment to protocol">
And the reported metric does not detect it: measured, fronto-parallel captures
give 0.184 px RMS with the focal length 650% wrong.
</Step>

<Step title="Which is why toolbox documentation is mostly about waving the board">
The geometry requirement, restated as an instruction.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Then time

<PaperCard refKey="furgale2013unified" />

The modern addition: sensors do not agree about *when*. Exposure, transfer and
buffering introduce offsets nobody specified, often tens of milliseconds, and
while the platform moves a fixed time offset looks exactly like a pose error that
varies with velocity.

<KeyIdea>
Estimating the offset jointly is the same move as putting a nuisance parameter in
the model rather than hoping it averages out — and the failure it prevents is the
one described in `states_observations`, where a camera 60 ms behind the encoders
puts the gripper 3 cm from where proprioception says it is. Spatial calibration
alone will absorb some of that into a compromise transform that is wrong in a
motion-dependent way, which is much harder to find than a constant offset.
</KeyIdea>
