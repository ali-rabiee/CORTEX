## Failure modes

Structured light fails on: transparent objects (IR passes through), specular surfaces (IR reflects away), outdoor scenes (IR sunlight interference), thin objects (insufficient pattern coverage). Stereo fails on: textureless regions (no features to match), repetitive patterns (ambiguous matching). Monocular fails on: metric scale (inherent ambiguity without known reference), unusual viewpoints not in training data. Symptoms: holes in depth maps, flying pixels at edges. Fix: multi-modal fusion (structured light + monocular), temporal filtering, confidence-weighted depth completion.

## Interview-ready answer

Depth estimation is the bridge between 2D perception and 3D robot interaction. In practice, I use a layered approach: structured light sensors (RealSense) provide reliable metric depth for most surfaces. For failure cases (transparent, specular), I fuse with monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything V2) after scale-shift alignment. The key engineering decision is sensor selection: structured light for indoor manipulation ($0.3$-$3\,\text{m}$), stereo for longer range, time-of-flight for outdoor. Monocular models have gotten remarkably good but still lack metric scale without calibration — I use them as a complement to active sensors, not a replacement. For production systems, always monitor depth validity masks and have fallback behaviors for low-confidence regions.
