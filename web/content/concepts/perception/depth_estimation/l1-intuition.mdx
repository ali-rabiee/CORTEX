## Intuition

A robot needs to know how far away things are to interact with them. Stereo vision works like human eyes — two slightly offset views let you triangulate distance. Structured light cheats by projecting a known pattern and seeing how it warps on surfaces. Monocular depth is the hardest: estimating 3D from a single 2D image, relying on learned cues like perspective, occlusion, and texture gradients — the same cues humans use when closing one eye.

## In practice

Depth pipeline for tabletop manipulation: Intel RealSense D435 (structured light, $1280 \times 720$ at 30 fps, range $0.3$-$3\,\text{m}$) for primary depth. For transparent objects where structured light fails, run Depth Anything V2 on the RGB image to get monocular depth, then fuse: $d_{\text{fused}}(u,v) = w \cdot d_{\text{sensor}}(u,v) + (1-w) \cdot \alpha \cdot d_{\text{mono}}(u,v) + \beta$, where $\alpha, \beta$ are scale/shift alignment parameters and $w$ is a confidence weight from the sensor validity mask.
